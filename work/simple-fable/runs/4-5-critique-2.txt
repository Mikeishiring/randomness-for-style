Critique of 4-5-2.html

ORIGINALITY — harsh read

1. The pit board rescued the motorsport half, but everything around it is
   coasting. Plate I is still a plain bar chart in green paint with one
   stripe: competent, honest, and the least original surface left. The
   header still spends its energy on the most obvious borrow (roundel)
   while the Victorian half of the header is carried by a single pinned
   tag. Neither flaw misleads a reader, so neither justifies churn — but
   they should be named: this design's originality now lives in three
   elements (board, beetles, pinned tag) and borrows convention everywhere
   else. For a performance report that is arguably correct; it is not brave.
2. The roundel "01" beside "Week 01" remains a double statement. Accepted
   again as a race-number echo; still the page's most tired device.
3. The fake Latin stays half-serious. With the beetles present it now has
   a visual anchor, so it reads as a consistent joke rather than a stray
   one. Kept.

CLARITY — harsh read

4. Real gap: the sources table never shows that its parts sum to the whole.
   A reader who cross-checks 5,240 + 4,480 + 2,760 against the "12,480"
   plaque has to do arithmetic the page should demonstrate. Parts-to-whole
   traceability is missing at the exact place it matters.
5. Real redundancy: the footer. "Synthetic specimen" repeats the pinned
   "Synthetic data" tag; "Collected Mon–Sun, Week 01" repeats the heading
   and the day axes. Two italic lines of atmosphere restating three things
   already on the page. Atmosphere that repeats information is filler.
6. The two plates use slightly different plot heights (170px vs 160px max).
   Invisible in practice because every mark is direct-labelled; no reader
   can misread it. Noted, not fixed — fixing it changes nothing a reader
   experiences.
7. Pit board: seven bare numbers demand a scan to see the rise. A trend
   encoding (scaled underlines, a spark) was considered and rejected: it
   would re-encode what Plates I–II already show and make the board
   busier; the board's job is exact per-day figures, and it does it.

CHANGES MADE IN 4-5-3.html

A. Totals row added to the sources table: full-width share track,
   12,480 visits, 624 signups, 5.0% conversion, set off by an ink rule.
   The breakdown now visibly reconciles with the plaques (addresses 4).
B. Footer removed entirely. The synthetic marker keeps its single, more
   prominent declaration (the pinned tag); the period keeps its heading;
   the days keep their axes (addresses 5).

CONSIDERED AND REJECTED

- Mon-to-Sun growth stat (+48% visits, +123% signups): the plates and the
  conversion board already demonstrate the divergence; a fifth plaque
  would state what is shown.
- Trend marks on the pit board (see 7).
- Replacing the roundel: identity continuity beats novelty at this point;
  swapping header furniture in round three is churn, not improvement.
- Equalising plate plot heights (see 6): zero reader-visible effect.

Everything else — purpose, data, every figure, plates, beetles, board,
plaques, palette, header — unchanged.
