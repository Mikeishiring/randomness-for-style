CRITIQUE OF 3-0-1.html

ORIGINALITY
- The government-form pastiche is the first idea anyone reaches for with
  "bureaucracy": a FORM number, Part I/II black bands, numbered boxes, a
  rubber stamp. It is executed competently, but it is a costume borrowed
  whole from the IRS, not a synthesis of two ingredients.
- Tech barely exists. Courier reads as typewriter -- bureaucracy again --
  and the background grid is so faint it is subliminal. One ingredient ate
  the other.
- The bars are stock dashboard bars: soft tinted tracks, a modern SaaS
  convention. They belong to neither identity and dilute the hard-ruled
  language of the rest of the page.
- "SYNTHETIC DATA" as a rubber stamp is the most obvious provenance gag
  available. Honest, but zero invention.

CLARITY
- Part I's signup bars have no column header and no values: an anonymous
  red bar, silently normalized to a different maximum than the visits bars
  beside it. It invites ratio misreading and scans as decoration.
- The mobile stylesheet hides the fourth column of every table. In Part II
  that column is the actual signups data. Data loss on phones -- a bug,
  not a trade-off.
- Red/blue conversion coloring is never explained. Red "4.0%" implies
  failure when it is merely the week minimum. The footer legend explains
  bar hues, not these.
- The totals row fakes alignment with a left-aligned span inside an empty
  bar container, so the weekly total does not sit under the daily values.
- An empty <th> sits over the signup bars; no scope attributes anywhere.

KEPT (strong decisions)
- Form frame and masthead, numbered summary boxes, black part bands,
  ledger tables with dotted leaders, peak-row highlight, the stamp's
  honesty about synthetic data, tabular monospace figures, and the derived
  share/conversion columns.

CHANGES APPLIED
1. Each signups number+bar merged into one labeled "Signups" column with
   its value shown; every bar now has a header and a number; the empty
   header is gone.
2. Bars redrawn as hard-ruled, segmented dot-matrix fills on paper tracks:
   one mark language that reads as both printout (tech) and tally
   (bureaucracy), replacing the generic dashboard bars.
3. Tech developed explicitly: a CSS barcode strip captioned with the
   week's control figures, and a terminal face (Consolas) for the machine
   layer -- box numbers, barcode caption, and the footer record line
   ("REV 2 - EOF") -- against the typewriter face of the form layer.
4. Conversion week-high/low now marked with ^ / v glyph prefixes plus a
   legend; color is no longer the only signal.
5. Mobile no longer hides data columns; bars compress instead.
6. Totals right-aligned under their columns with ordinary cells; scope
   attributes added to headers and row labels.
Purpose, figures, table structure and all derived values unchanged.
