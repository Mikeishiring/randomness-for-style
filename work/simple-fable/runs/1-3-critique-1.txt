Critique of 1-3-1.html (harsh, originality and clarity), followed by the changes applied in 1-3-2.html.

ORIGINALITY

1. The identity is mostly a classical-luxury veneer. Gold on velvet, a high-contrast serif,
   a meander band: that combination decorates any upscale restaurant menu or perfume box.
   Only two decisions are actually original — visits drawn as fluted marble columns and
   signups drawn as noteheads on a staff — and both stop at the chart. The header and the
   sources table carry no developed identity beyond palette.

2. "Greek myths" is not in the page at all. Columns, meander, laurel are Greek
   *architecture and ornament*; there is no myth. Worse, the laurel sprig is the single
   most generic classical sign available. The obvious fusion was missed: opera was born
   from Greek myth — the first operas (Peri's Euridice, Monteverdi's L'Orfeo) are the
   Orpheus story, and the lyre inside a laurel wreath is the historic opera-house crest.
   One emblem could have carried both ingredients at once; instead the page hedges with
   stock garnish.

3. The opera ingredient is reduced to a curtain and five gridlines. The "staff" is a dot
   plot wearing a costume: the noteheads have no stems, so at a glance they read as
   ordinary round markers and the musical conceit evaporates. The curtain's swags are
   uniform and flat — closer to clipart than to drawn fabric.

4. The playbill framing ("Week 01" as a production) is asserted by typography and then
   abandoned: nothing else on the page treats the seven days as seven nights of a run.

CLARITY

5. The gold accent meaning "peak" (Sunday column cap, label, value, conversion) is never
   established; a reader must infer it. Tolerable — the tooltip says "peak day" — but it
   is an implicit convention doing real work.

6. In the sources table, the signups segment is drawn at the same scale as visits — the
   honest choice — but the legend says only "of which signups". At Social's 1.6% the gold
   sliver is about five pixels and can read as a rendering artifact rather than a segment.
   The track has no boundary, so bar end and sliver have nothing to register against.

7. Structural fragility: the bottom meander band fills with url(#fret), a pattern defined
   inside the *header* SVG. Same-document references across inline SVGs work today, but
   the band silently vanishes if the header is removed or the reference handling changes.

8. The Didot/Bodoni stack degrades to Times New Roman on most Windows machines, losing
   much of the playbill voice. Unfixable while self-contained (no linked fonts); the
   letter-spacing and scale carry the identity in the fallback. Accepted constraint.

CONSIDERED AND REJECTED (clarity would have paid for identity)

- Renaming sections or days as "Act I–VII" / "The Chorus": acts belong inside one opera,
  and operatic names would displace the labels a reader needs. Rejected as primary labels;
  added only as subdued secondary numerals under the columns (the days stay primary).
- A crescendo hairpin under the melody: true in aggregate (signups swell 57 → 127) but it
  editorializes past the Thursday dip. The per-day conversion strip already tells it.
- Mythic epithets for Direct/Search/Social: obscures the only names the owner uses.
- Any added prose (epigraph, takeaway line): the figures and accents already demonstrate
  the story; copy would be filler.

CHANGES APPLIED IN 1-3-2.html (everything else preserved: data, layout, both charts'
scales and values, stats, table figures, tooltips)

a. Masthead emblem: the laurel sprigs flanking the title are replaced by one composed
   crest — a lyre within a laurel wreath — the Orpheus emblem that fuses Greek myth with
   opera. Drawn inline, decorative, aria-hidden.
b. Noteheads gain short down-stems so the signups panel unmistakably reads as a melody,
   not a scatter. Stems are equal length and encode nothing.
c. Subdued gold numerals I–VII sit between the column bases and the day labels: the seven
   nights of the run, secondary to Mon–Sun. Staff and conversion strip shift down to make
   room; all signup coordinates recomputed for the same 40–120 scale.
d. Table legend now says "signups · same scale", and the bar track gets a faint inset
   hairline so the Social sliver registers as a segment against a bounded bar.
e. The bottom meander band gets its own pattern definition (no cross-SVG dependency).
f. The curtain hem is doubled (a second, fainter trim line) for depth; swag geometry
   unchanged.

No figures, labels, scales, or claims changed. The revision develops both ingredients
where it is free and leaves the reading of the data exactly as it was.
