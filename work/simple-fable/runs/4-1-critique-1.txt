Critique of runs/4-1-1.html

ORIGINALITY

1. The fusion is decals, not design. A checker strip is the motorsport sticker,
   a disco ball is the disco sticker, and they are placed side by side without
   ever combining. No single element reads as both ingredients at once, so the
   identity feels assembled from clip-art rather than conceived.
2. The disco ball is pure ornament — the first cliche available — carrying no
   information and no trace of motorsport.
3. Pink-orange-gold gradient type with an animated sheen on near-black is stock
   synthwave dashboard styling. It is what every dark "fun" dashboard already
   does; neither disco (mirror-silver, sparkle, spotlights) nor motorsport
   (liveries, door numbers, telemetry) demanded this exact default.
4. The data displays are entirely generic bar charts. The identity lives only
   in the frame around the data; strip the header and footer and this could be
   any dashboard. The brief asked the identity not to compete with the
   information, but absence from the information is not the same thing.
5. "Finishing order" is the one genuinely fused idea and it stops at a chip.
   The race metaphor never touches the interesting story — Direct converts at
   roughly twice Social's rate — and conversion gets no visual treatment at all.

CLARITY

6. Inconsistent chart labeling: "Visits" sits above its bars while "Signups"
   sits below its bars, so the reader meets the gold bars before their label.
   Worse, the two bar rows share one container and baseline rhythm, implying a
   shared scale when the scales differ by roughly 16x.
7. Gold means two things at once: signups (bar color) and "peak day" (Sunday's
   recolored visits bar, value, and day label). Sunday's white-gold visits bar
   can be parsed as a different metric rather than emphasis.
8. Source stats are inline dim text per row. Numbers do not align vertically
   across sources, so comparing signups or conversion across the three rows
   means re-reading each sentence-like line. The unit words repeated three
   times ("visits", "signups", "conversion") are copy that alignment deletes.
9. The source track encodes visits only — information the P-chip and the
   number already give twice — while conversion, the most decision-relevant
   figure on the page, is never encoded.

CHANGES MADE IN 4-1-2

- Fused the two marks into one: the disco ball's facets are now a chequered-
  flag pattern, a single element that is simultaneously mirror ball and
  chequered flag. The generic facet grid is gone.
- Peak day: replaced the gold recolor with a chequered finish-line cap on
  Sunday's bars in both charts, with white emphasis on its values and day
  label. Gold now means signups only, and the finish line carries meaning in
  identity language: the week finishes at its peak.
- Daily charts: both row labels now sit above their bars, each chart has its
  own baseline with clear separation so the differing scales no longer share
  an implied axis, and the row labels take their metric's color (silver for
  visits, gold for signups), tying the coding together without a legend.
- Sources: rebuilt as one aligned leaderboard — a single column-header row
  (Visits / Signups / Conv.), right-aligned tabular numbers, signups in gold
  to match the chart coding, and the repeated per-row unit copy deleted.
  Conversion is now bold foreground ink, the strongest number in each row.
  The visits track remains as the visual rationale for the finishing order.

PRESERVED, DELIBERATELY

The KPI row; direct value labels on every bar (they replace axes); the
P1/P2/P3 podium; the dark palette and condensed italic type; the synthetic-
data badge; and the gradient title with its restrained, reduced-motion-safe
sheen — the strongest disco gesture on the page. Purpose, figures, and data
are unchanged.
