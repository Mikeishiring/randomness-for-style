CRITIQUE OF 3-3-2 (harsh, originality and clarity)

ORIGINALITY

1. The structure is inconsistent with its own conceit. Acts I and II get
   playbill headers; the opening block of four figures — the overture of any
   programme — gets nothing. The page invents a theatrical frame and then
   forgets to seat its first section in it.
2. "The cast" earned its title but not its costume. The rows are still a
   plain ledger grid. Playbills and bureaucratic indexes share one signature
   device — the dotted leader carrying the eye from name to figure — and the
   design missed this rare piece of genuinely shared vocabulary between its
   two ingredients for a second time.
3. Bureaucracy without a sum line is theatre. The most canonical element of
   any official ledger — the ruled TOTAL row that proves the parts account
   for the whole — is absent. Its absence is not minimalism; it drops real
   information (that the three sources fully reconcile with the week's
   12,480 visits and 624 signups, which the page currently leaves the reader
   to verify by mental arithmetic).
4. The verification ritual still rests entirely on one rubber stamp. Field
   numbers and a form number exist, but no mark closes the document's loop.
   (A second stamp would be repetition, not development; the sum line is the
   better closing mark and is the change made.)

CLARITY

1. The bar column in the cast list has no scale reference. Bars are
   proportional to the longest (Direct), but nothing shows the shared
   measure, so Search's 85.5% of Direct must be judged against empty paper.
2. The bar column's header cell is empty. Tolerable — each bar sits beside
   its printed figure — but it is the one column a stranger must infer.
3. Four figures at the top are weekly totals, but nothing says so; "12,480
   visits" could be misread as something other than the seven-day sum until
   the reader cross-checks Act I.
4. "In triplicate" remains the page's one line of pure joke. Considered
   replacing it with scope data and rejected: every candidate ("Week 01",
   "synthetic") already appears once, and a redundant label is worse than a
   two-word piece of identity texture. Retained deliberately.
5. Considered and rejected: a fermata or star over Sunday's peak (glyph
   support is unreliable and a floating mark reads as a footnote); a header
   for the bar column (noise next to self-labelling bars).

CHANGES APPLIED (one pass)

- Overture seated in the frame: the figures block now opens with the same
  header furniture as the acts — "Overture · The week in sum" — fixing the
  structural inconsistency and stating plainly that these are weekly totals.
- Dotted leaders laid under the cast bars: a dotted track now runs the full
  column width behind each visits bar, playbill-style, carrying the eye from
  name to figure and doubling as the shared-scale reference the bars lacked.
- The ledger closed with a sum line: a double-ruled TOTAL row (12,480 · 624 ·
  5.0%) ends Act II, reconciling sources against the overture's totals — the
  bureaucratic ritual that was missing, carrying real verification value.

PRESERVED (strong decisions)

- The measure-ruled three-register score, printed values in place of axes,
  the consistent "signups per visit" vocabulary, the synthetic-data stamp,
  the numbered overture fields, the curtain with folds and scallops, the
  docket, act titles, FIN. All figures, sums and ratios unchanged; the only
  new numbers are the totals row, which restates existing sums where a
  ledger requires them.
