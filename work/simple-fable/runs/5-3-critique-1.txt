Critique of 5-3-1.html

ORIGINALITY

1. "Dark page with glowing cyan bars" is the default dark-mode dashboard, not
   deep-sea bioluminescence. The abyss does no work: there is no depth, no
   light-from-the-surface, and the 6px maroon border-top is an invisible token
   gesture at a curtain. Swap the labels and this could ship as any stock dark
   theme.

2. Opera is asserted by vocabulary ("Act I", "Act II") and a double rule, not
   by design. With no loaded fonts the stack lands on Georgia for nearly every
   viewer, which has none of the high-contrast Didone letterforms the playbill
   gesture depends on. The identity rests on two words and an italic title.

3. The two ingredients never actually combine; they run in parallel colour
   channels (cyan = sea, gold = gilt) with no shared idea. The data itself
   offers the fusion — every measure rises all week to a Sunday peak, a
   crescendo to a finale — and the design never stages it.

4. The signup orbs are the one invented element, and they fail as an encoding:
   the halo/core double circle makes the size ambiguous, and the halo radii
   span only 13–20px, so the row reads as decoration with numbers attached.

CLARITY

5. Graphical integrity bug in Sources: the visits bar is scaled to 5,240 =
   full width and the signups bar directly beneath it to 314 = full width.
   Adjacent bars on different scales invite a false read — Direct looks as if
   signups nearly match visits when they are 6% of them. The printed numbers
   rescue the reader; the picture still lies.

6. The most useful fact for the owner — conversion climbing 4.0% to 6.0%
   across the week, Sunday best on every measure — appears nowhere. Only the
   weekly 5.0% average is shown.

7. At phone width the 940-unit viewBox scales the 14px value labels to about
   5px: unreadable, with no mitigation.

8. Nothing tells the reader that orb size means anything; the "Signups"
   caption labels the row, not the encoding.

CHANGES APPLIED IN 5-3-2.html

a. Sources bars now share one scale (percent of the largest source's visits).
   The signup slivers are honest and show, visually, that roughly one visit in
   twenty converts; sliver-to-bar proportion now mirrors source quality. The
   conversion column stays.
b. Added a quiet per-day conversion row (4.0% → 6.0%) under the signups row:
   the crescendo becomes visible data, not prose.
c. Orbs rebuilt as single circles with area proportional to signups
   (r = 22·√(s/127)), a faint ring marking the true radius; the range widens
   to 14.7–22px and the encoded size is no longer ambiguous.
d. Staged the finale: a soft vertical shaft of light behind Sunday's column —
   stage spotlight and surface light in one device, anchored to the data peak.
e. Replaced the flat maroon line with a scalloped velvet curtain edge with a
   gold hem (pure CSS) and flanked the overline with small fleurons: opera
   made physical instead of verbal.
f. The chart keeps a 760px minimum width and scrolls horizontally on narrow
   screens so every label stays legible.

PRESERVED (strong decisions): the playbill header with double rules, the KPI
triad, printed values on every mark, the cast-list source rows with the
conversion column, the act labels, and the copy level — no new prose was
added, only data and captions.
