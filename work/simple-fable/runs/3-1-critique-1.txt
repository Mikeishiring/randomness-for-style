CRITIQUE OF 3-1-1.html

ORIGINALITY — harsh

1. Disco is a coat of paint, not a structure. Strip the palette and nothing
   disco remains: a gradient headline, gradient bars and a diagonal stripe are
   stock "fun dashboard" moves that could ship under any party brand. The one
   genuinely disco object, the mirror ball, is a 52px garnish floating in the
   header, connected to nothing.
2. The diagonal stripe divider is a hazard-tape cliche. It reads
   "construction site", not "dance floor".
3. The smooth multi-stop rainbow on the title is the generic 2020s gradient
   text treatment. Actual 70s disco lettering has a signature the page
   ignores: chrome with a hard horizon line.
4. Bureaucracy stays at costume level. Form number, section strips, margin
   rule, punched holes and a stamp decorate the boxes, but the form never
   shapes how data is presented. The strongest bureaucratic devices — the
   fill-in entry line with the figure typed ON the rule, dotted leaders,
   tabulated returns — go unused. The totals cells with a floating 44px
   underline could come from any template.
5. The two ingredients never touch. Every element is either a form element or
   a disco element; the only fusion is a pink margin rule. The available
   original move — bureaucratic machinery rendering disco material (entries
   filed on chrome rules, bars built from mirror tiles, a lit floor strip as
   the divider) — was left on the table.

CLARITY — harsh

6. The Mon–Sun axis is printed twice, once under each chart. A full duplicate
   row that forces the eye to re-anchor and adds no information.
7. The signup-rate row floats at the bottom with its own caption and no
   visible tie to the day columns; on narrow screens the reader counts cells
   to map 5.4% to Sat.
8. The "■" legend swatches are color keys for single-series charts whose
   caption already names the series and whose bars sit directly below. Dead
   ink.
9. The peak-day sub-line "2,100 · 127" makes the reader infer what the two
   numbers are.
10. "Section 1" spells out what the numbered strip already says; bureaucratic
    registers abbreviate (§).

REVISION APPLIED

- Title re-lettered as 70s chrome: hard horizon gradient (violet/pink sky,
  gold ground) instead of the generic smooth rainbow.
- Divider rebuilt as a lit dance-floor strip: square color tiles with ink
  grout, replacing the hazard stripe.
- All bars (daily and source) now carry a faint mirror-tile facet grid over
  the gradient — the mirror ball becomes the material the data is drawn
  with, at a contrast low enough not to disturb length reading.
- Totals rebuilt as filled-in form entries: each figure sits on a full-width
  chrome-gradient entry rule, label above, like a typed return — the two
  ingredients in one element.
- Daily charts merged onto ONE shared day axis: visits rise above the ruled
  Mon–Sun band, signups hang below it (each metric keeps its own scale and
  every bar keeps its printed value, so no scales are conflated). The
  duplicate axis row is gone and each day's pair reads in one vertical line.
- Signup-rate row now sits directly under the signup bars in the same column
  grid, separated by a single rule.
- Legend swatches removed; captions alone name the series.
- Peak-day sub-line now reads "2,100 visits · 127 signups".
- Section heads tightened to "§ 1 · TOTALS" etc.

PRESERVED

Form WK-01 / Period line, synthetic-data stamp as the caveat, punched filing
holes and pink margin rule, mirror ball, the totals/by-day/by-source
structure, every supplied and derived figure, printed values on all bars, the
source table with per-source rate, and the Sunday highlight that matches the
peak-day entry. Purpose and data unchanged; page remains fully
self-contained.
