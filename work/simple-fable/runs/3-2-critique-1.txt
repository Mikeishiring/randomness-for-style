CRITIQUE OF 3-2-1.html

ORIGINALITY — harsh read

1. The bureaucracy is surface chrome. A form tag, one stamp, and numbered
   section headers sit on top of what is otherwise a generic dashboard:
   four KPI cards, a bar chart, three horizontal bar rows. Swap the palette
   and nothing below the header would tell you this page had an identity
   at all. The form never reaches the data: no printed scales, no ledger
   rows, no item numbering, no filled-in-field feel where the numbers live.

2. Fast food is carried almost entirely by color plus one awning. Ketchup
   red and mustard yellow without any fast-food FORM is just red and
   yellow. Nothing in the data display evokes the genre: no receipt, no
   ticket, no counter, no menu-board logic.

3. The two ingredients are adjacent, not fused. Awning on top, form below,
   and they never touch. The obvious fused move — official paperwork ABOUT
   a fast-food operation, e.g. totals printed as a register receipt that
   then gets rubber-stamped — was left on the table.

4. Impact for the display face is the laziest possible choice. It reads as
   meme/discount-flyer, which is neither bureaucratic nor fast food, and it
   fights the Courier that does all the actual identity work. The
   two-tone "hot word" in the title is a decorator's tic, not a decision.

5. The one genuinely fused element — red "CONV" stamp boxes in the sources
   ledger — is repeated identically three times, so it reads as a style,
   not a stamp.

CLARITY — harsh read

1. The mirrored daily chart uses independent scales above and below the
   baseline and never says so. The signup bars render at roughly 40% of
   the visits bars, visually implying signups are ~40% of visits; the true
   ratio is 5%. The printed numbers rescue it, but a chart that needs
   rescuing by its labels is a clarity defect.

2. The per-day conversion figure floats unanchored under the signup
   number. The "CONV %" legend chip has no swatch and no position, so the
   reader must infer what the muted percentage belongs to.

3. The peak-day marker (red-filled SUN label) collides with the color
   code: red means signups everywhere else. The marker is also never
   explained anywhere.

4. In the sources ledger the mustard and red bars are scaled to different
   maxima, again unstated, inviting the same cross-metric misreading.

5. Signup numbers hang at ragged heights under ragged bars, so the eye
   cannot scan them as a row.

6. "DAILY SERVICE RECORD" is flavor at the cost of plainness.

WHAT IS STRONG AND KEPT

Manila form sheet with hard offset shadow; the awning; one stamp carrying
both SYNTHETIC DATA and WEEK 01; mustard=visits / red=signups encoding;
a number printed on every bar; conversion computed at every level; peak
day surfaced; all figures from the supplied data only; fully static and
self-contained.

CHANGES APPLIED IN 3-2-2.html

1. Fused the ingredients where they were only adjacent: the KPI card row
   is now a register receipt (white slip, dotted leaders, dashed tear
   rule, torn bottom edge) whose bottom "total" line is the 5.0%
   conversion, and the SYNTHETIC DATA / WEEK 01 stamp is struck across
   the receipt's corner — stamped paperwork about a fast-food counter.
   Same four figures, same detail (peak day keeps 2,100 visits and 6.0%).
2. Dropped Impact entirely. The title is now two stacked signage bars —
   white-on-ketchup and ink-on-mustard — set in the same bold Courier as
   the rest of the form, so one typeface carries both ingredients.
3. Gave the daily chart a printed axis column, like a ledger stub:
   "VISITS 0–2,100" against the upper band, "SIGNUPS 0–127" against the
   lower band, and a "CONV %" row label. Dual scales are now declared on
   the page instead of discovered, and the conversion figures are
   anchored to a named row (values lose their repeated % signs).
4. Aligned signup numbers and conversion figures into fixed rows under
   the baseline so they scan as table rows; bureaucratic and clearer.
5. Peak-day marker recolored from red to ink so it no longer collides
   with the signups encoding; the receipt names SUN in plain text.
6. Sources rows gained form item numbers (2A, 2B, 2C) and the color
   legend moved to that section's header, the first place colors appear
   without an adjacent axis label.
7. "DAILY SERVICE RECORD" renamed to "DAILY RECORD".

Data, purpose, structure of sections, bar proportions, and all computed
figures are unchanged.
