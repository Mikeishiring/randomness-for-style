CRITIQUE — "Site Weather, Week 01" (2-0-1.html)

ORIGINALITY
1. The forecast strip is an iOS-weather pastiche, not an invented identity. Day label /
   glyph / big number / temperature curve is lifted wholesale; the page borrows a familiar
   surface instead of synthesizing one. The single genuinely original move — weather
   conditions encode CONVERSION, not traffic — is buried: it lives in a 10px legend at the
   bottom of the card while the layout implies the stock reading (icon summarizes the big
   number beneath it).
2. The "Tech" ingredient is generic dark-dashboard dressing: monospace digits,
   letter-spaced caps, a dot grid, a dashed chip. A hundred dashboards do this. Weather and
   Tech coexist on the page; they never combine into one idea.
3. The identity evaporates in the Sources card: a plain bar table with three small icons
   attached to the wrong element (the source name, not the conversion figure they encode).
4. The sun glyph is used four ways (wordmark, header stat, legend, source row) with no
   hierarchy of meaning.

CLARITY
5. Inverted hierarchy: the icons are driven by conversion, yet per-day conversion is the
   smallest, dimmest number on the page (10.5px, bottom row), two bands away from the icons
   it explains. A reader must find the legend to avoid misreading the glyphs as "visits
   weather".
6. The legend is a sentence plus four decimal-noise thresholds ("4.25–4.75%") — honest, but
   written as prose instead of the scale it actually is.
7. The amber area fill under the visits curve hangs from a 1,400 baseline. The line and its
   point labels are honest; the fill implies magnitude from a false zero. Decoration should
   not borrow the shape of evidence.
8. The strip scrolls horizontally on phones with no affordance; content silently clips.
9. The sources table's background bars encode visits, but nothing says which column the bar
   belongs to.
10. Markup nit: thead cells override fonts with inline style="font:inherit".

REFINEMENTS APPLIED (one pass)
- Conversion promoted into the condition row: each day now reads DAY / glyph / conv% as one
  unit, with micro band labels CONV / VISITS / SIGNUPS down the left edge. The metaphor's
  rule is visible where it operates, and the strip reads as three labeled instrument bands.
- Legend rebuilt as an instrument: a slate-to-amber conversion scale (3.0–6.5%) with the
  four condition glyphs sitting over their bands and threshold ticks at 4.25 / 4.75 / 5.25.
  It reads as a scale, not a sentence.
- Area fill replaced by a narrow glow stroke along the visits line — emphasis without a
  false-baseline area.
- The strip now sits on an inset "instrument screen" panel with edge scroll shadows: the
  Tech half gains a purpose (telemetry screen) and narrow viewports get a visible cue that
  the strip scrolls.
- Sources: the condition icon moved next to the conversion value it encodes; an amber
  swatch in the VISITS column header keys the row bars; the inline-style hack replaced by a
  class.
- Header H/L readings now name their day (H 2,100 SUN · L 1,420 MON) — weather-authentic
  and traceable to the strip.

PRESERVED (deliberately)
- Night-sky palette, mono numerals, direct point labels, bar-behind-row sources table, H/L
  framing, the SYNTHETIC chip, the static no-JS page, and every supplied figure and derived
  rate. These carried the first version; they are developed here, not replaced.
- Horizontal scroll on phones is kept (now signaled) rather than re-stacking the strip
  vertically: a full mobile re-layout risks more clarity than it buys in a single pass.
