Critique of runs/2-3-1.html (Weather + Opera, Week 01)

ORIGINALITY
1. The "opera" half is mostly generic luxury styling: midnight blue, gold
   hairlines, Didot small caps, a diamond ornament. That vocabulary says
   perfume ad or hotel menu as readily as opera. The only genuinely operatic
   element is the crescendo hairpin, an 18px unlabeled decoration most
   readers will take for a divider.
2. The "weather" half is the stock forecast icon set pasted above a wholly
   conventional grouped bar chart. The icons restate the bars directly
   beneath them (same variable, coarser resolution), so the two identities
   sit side by side instead of fusing. Neither ingredient shapes the
   structure of the report; both are applied as skin.
3. Daytime sun glyphs contradict the opera's night-time register. The two
   ingredients were not reconciled even at the level of time of day.
4. The identity evaporates below the fold: "By source" is a plain bar table
   with no trace of either ingredient.

CLARITY
5. Integrity defect: visits and signups bars share one baseline but are each
   normalized to their own maximum. On Sunday both read 100% height,
   visually asserting parity between 2,100 and 127. The sources section
   stacks the same two-scale trick in adjacent rows.
6. The glyph encoding (sky = relative visits) is never declared; the icons
   could be mistaken for actual weather, a fabricated-looking claim on a
   data page.
7. The crescendo hairpin asserts monotonic growth; Thursday dips in both
   metrics, so the flourish overstates the data.
8. Gold means three things at once: signups (per the legend), sunshine (in
   the glyphs), and brand trim (rules, headings) - a direct encoding
   collision.
9. The "Conversion" column header is 9px and letterspaced to
   near-illegibility, and the per-day percentages are unlabeled.

REFINEMENT APPLIED (same two ingredients, developed structurally)
- Fused the metaphors into the chart's architecture: the daily chart is now
  an opera-house cross-section. Visits perform on the stage (silver bars
  above the stage line); signups glow in the orchestra pit (gold strips
  mirrored below the line); day and conversion sit beneath the pit. Band
  labels on the left name each register, replacing the chip legend.
- One honest scale: pit strips and both source bars now share the same
  pixels-per-visit scale as the visits bars, so gold/silver lengths show
  true magnitude and conversion reads as a visible proportion. Printed
  values preserve exact detail where the honest bars are small.
- Night sky, not daytime: the glyphs become a clearing night - drizzle,
  cloud, moon behind cloud, full moon with stars - matching the opera's
  register and freeing gold to mean signups only (moons are ivory).
- Declared the sky encoding with a one-line key (drizzle -> moon: "visits,
  low to high").
- Removed the crescendo hairpin as unearned and mildly misleading. The
  opera identity is instead carried by a crimson curtain valance across the
  top of the sheet - decoration that asserts nothing about the data.
- Completed and enlarged conversion labeling: a "Conv." band row label on
  the daily chart, full-size "Conversion" header in sources.

KEPT (strong decisions): playbill header and ornament rules, totals row,
exact figures and Mon-Sun order, synthetic-data flag, section structure,
self-contained system-font implementation.
