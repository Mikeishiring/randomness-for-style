Critique of 4-4-1.html

ORIGINALITY — weak. The first version is the laziest possible reading of both
ingredients. Motorsport is a 10px checkered sticker at the top of the page and
an italic font style; luxury hotel is Didot, letterspacing and gold hairlines —
the generic "premium dark" look of any whisky brand or barber shop. The two
ingredients never combine: they sit in separate CSS properties (serif = hotel,
italic = motorsport) instead of fusing into one device. Motorsport has a rich,
data-native graphic language — timing sheets, sector splits, fastest-lap
marking, number plates — and the data (seven days, a peak day, a best
conversion) maps straight onto it; none of it was used. Hotel language beyond
the font — folio ledgers, ruled registers, room-number plaques — was equally
untouched.

CLARITY — compromised in four places.
1. Daily signups were a detached row of gold numbers floating below the day
   labels; they read as footnotes, not data, and the legend swatch pointed at a
   2.5px gold cap on each bar that encoded nothing. A legend describing
   decoration is dishonest.
2. Conversion-by-day lived in a separate panel, forcing the reader to
   cross-reference Sunday's 2,100 visits against Sunday's 6.0% two charts
   apart. Worse, its line sat on a 3.5% baseline, visually exaggerating a
   4.0→6.0 climb; the axis was labeled, but small.
3. The sources table interleaved orphan bar rows under the figures, doubling
   the row count, and the bar (visits only) stretched under all four columns —
   ambiguous scope.
4. Sunday's bar used a lighter green with no stated meaning, and gold carried
   three meanings at once (signups, conversion, decoration).

STRONG DECISIONS PRESERVED: the deep racing-green/champagne palette, the Didot
nameplate with condensed italic numerals, the KPI tiles with gold stripes, the
"Synthetic data" badge, minimal copy, zero JavaScript, system fonts only.

CHANGES MADE:
- Fused device: both data sections become one shared grammar — a race timing
  sheet typeset as a hotel folio ledger. Each day/source is one ruled row with
  the exact figure and a zero-based inline bar per metric, scaled to the
  column maximum. All three daily metrics now sit on one line; no
  cross-referencing, no truncated baseline, no unexplained emphasis.
- Conversion integrated per day (and per source) with its own zero-based bar;
  the separate line-chart panel and its 3.5% baseline are gone.
- Disciplined color roles: green bars = visits, gold bars = signups, champagne
  bars = conversion; all figures ivory. The fake legend is gone.
- Peak day marked explicitly: Sunday row highlighted with a small checkered
  flag — the checker motif now means something (finish/peak) instead of being
  only a border sticker.
- Header monogram: "01" in a gold-bordered plaque — race number plate and
  hotel room plaque in one mark; the title dedupes to "Website Performance"
  (the period lives in the plaque, KPI context and footer).
- Section panels carry a double livery stripe echoing the KPI tiles.
- Same data, same purpose, still fully self-contained with no external
  requests and no scripts.
