CRITIQUE OF 4-3-2.html

ORIGINALITY
1. The page still takes turns instead of singing a duet. The header is pure
   playbill; motorsport only arrives in the details below it. The strongest
   fusion on the page - the checkered finish line inside the sources chart -
   shows what the rest lacks: elements that read both ways at once. Most other
   elements remain one-genre quotations placed side by side.
2. The heading "Sources - Ranked by visits" explains but does not perform. The
   design already runs a racing fiction (grid numerals, finish line); a plain
   analyst's label breaks character where "finishing order" would keep it and
   say exactly the same thing.
3. The checker ribbons are still the most generic items on the page. After the
   finish line moved into the data display, the top and bottom ribbons restate
   the motif as pure trim.
4. Opera thins out below the fold. In Sources, the operatic contribution has
   shrunk to a typeface; programme billing conventions (italicized names, act
   numerals - only the numerals were used) sit unused.
5. The red "fastest lap" system is the right instinct executed timidly: a 2px
   cap at the very top of a 190px bar is nearly invisible, so the system
   exists mostly in the author's head.

CLARITY
6. Red is asserted, never taught. Nothing identifies it as "best of week";
   because the caps are easy to miss, the one cross-cutting insight the color
   carries (Sunday peaks everything; Direct converts best) risks reading as
   decoration - the exact fault the previous pass was meant to fix.
7. Inconsistent highlighting: among the week's three peak figures, only the
   6.0 conversion values are red; 2,100 and 127 look no different from their
   neighbors even though they end the same story.
8. Minor: the daily block mixes two chart rows and one numbers-only row under
   parallel labels; tolerable and honestly labeled.
9. No overflow or mobile defects found in this version; the auto-sizing
   columns fixed the previous fault.

CHANGES APPLIED IN 4-3-3.html
- Red made legible, not just present: Sunday's printed peaks (2,100 visits,
  127 signups) are now set in the highlight red to match their caps and the
  already-red 6.0 figures, and the winning caps are a step thicker. Red now
  reads consistently as "best of the week" without spending a legend on it.
- Sources heading recast as "Finishing order - By visits": same information,
  stays in character, and names what the numerals and bars mean.
- Source names set in italic serif, the programme-billing convention,
  restoring the opera half of the identity in the lower page at zero cost to
  reading.

KEPT, DESPITE THE CRITIQUE
- Checker ribbons: redundant as information, kept as frame. The opening
  marquee and the closing flag bookend the report - the week "finishes" at
  the foot of the page - and removing them would cost more structure than
  their cliche costs taste.
- Plain rectangular bars: curtain-shaped or slanted bars were considered and
  rejected; distorting data-bearing shapes trades clarity for theme.
- Everything else from 4-3-2: structure, palette, playbill header, roman
  numerals, finish line, conversion row, and every supplied figure unchanged.
