Critique of runs/4-1-3.html

ORIGINALITY

1. The mirror chart is the page's one real idea inside the data; everywhere
   else the identity is still perimeter dressing — ball, trim, chips. Three
   rounds in, the gradient-sheen title has never been re-examined; it remains
   the stock dark-dashboard default it was on day one.
2. The leaderboard talks race but races only one metric. For a "finishing
   order" conceit, the gap to the leader is the obvious drawing to make, and
   the page's actual story — Social brings over half of Direct's traffic but
   barely a quarter of its signups — sits in small numbers no eye will race.
3. Disco still leans entirely on gold, gradient, and the ball; motorsport on
   checker, italics, and chips. The fused moments (chequered facets, finish-
   line caps, the equalizer mirror) are good but finite; nothing new was
   ventured in round three's chrome.

CLARITY

4. The mirror reintroduced the complaint it was meant to bury, and made it
   worse where it matters most: in DOM order the "Signups" label comes dead
   last, so a screen reader hears seven visits numbers, seven day names, then
   seven bare gold numbers — and only afterwards the word that explains them.
   The chart is a riddle in audio.
5. Every bar value is a bare number associated with its day only by column
   position. Visually fine; in audio the mapping does not exist at all.
6. If background-clip:text is unsupported, color:transparent leaves the page
   title invisible. A report whose headline can vanish is a robustness hole,
   however rare the browser.
7. The sighted entry-order cost remains mild but real: hanging gold bars are
   met before their bottom-edge label. The symmetric outer labels are the
   right convention for a mirror, but they carry no cue that direction is the
   organizing idea.
8. The peak-day KPI names visits only, though Sunday also peaks signups (127).

CHANGES IN 4-1-4

- Sources: added a thin gold signups track under each visits track, each
  metric normalized to its own leader — a gap-to-leader read in race language.
  The conversion story is now drawn, not just printed: Social's gold bar
  collapses to 27.4% while its visits bar holds 52.7% (fixes 2; visits stays
  pink-orange, signups gold, consistent with the chart coding).
- Mirror chart, assistive order: both bar groups now carry group labels
  ("Visits by day", "Signups by day") announced before their numbers, every
  value gets a visually hidden day prefix ("Sun 2,100"), and the decorative
  visible labels are removed from the accessibility tree (fixes 4, 5).
- Mirror chart, sighted entry cue: the metric labels gained direction markers
  — "Visits [up]" above, "Signups [down]" below — declaring the mirror's
  organizing axis at both edges (addresses 7).
- Title: the gradient-and-transparent treatment now sits behind an @supports
  guard, falling back to solid ink where background-clip:text is unsupported;
  the headline can no longer vanish (fixes 6).

CONSIDERED AND REJECTED

A daily conversion row inside the day band (4.0% rising to 6.0%) would add a
real insight but needs a labeled left gutter that collapses the seven columns
on phone widths, or sits unlabeled and ambiguous; the sources table and KPI
carry conversion instead. Widening the peak-day KPI to name both peaks (8)
overflows the tile at two-column phone width; the chart's finish-line caps
already mark both. The KPI cards stay plain and the sheen stays: after three
rounds the page needs restraint more than another gesture (1, 3 noted, not
acted on). Purpose, figures, and data are unchanged.
