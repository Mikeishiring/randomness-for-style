CRITIQUE OF 3-3-1 (harsh, originality and clarity)

ORIGINALITY

1. The blend is lopsided: almost all bureaucracy, with opera as set dressing.
   The opera contribution is a flat maroon band, a scalloped strip, the word
   "Act", an italic Didone, and "FIN". None of it uses opera's real visual
   language — a proscenium with depth, playbill hierarchy, a cast list. The
   curtain reads as a generic colored header, not a curtain.
2. Cliché inventory: the rubber stamp is the laziest bureaucracy signifier
   available, the "✦" flanks are stock ornament, and four stat boxes in a row
   is the default dashboard move with a double outline painted on. Little here
   could not be produced by asking for "old-timey government form".
3. The strongest original idea — Act I as two aligned registers with a ratio
   line, like staves in a score — is underdeveloped. It is actually just two
   bar charts stacked. There are no shared measure lines tying the registers
   into one instrument, which is exactly what would make the metaphor visible
   and earn the name "score".
4. Act II's title, "The sources", is a dead beat. The sources table is
   literally a cast list (three named performers, their houses drawn, their
   results tallied) and the design never notices.

CLARITY

1. "S ÷ V" appears twice (score register, ledger header) and is cryptic. A
   reader should not decode an invented abbreviation to learn the page's most
   important derived number. The same quantity is labelled three different
   ways across the page ("Signups / Visit", "S ÷ V"), which is worse.
2. Mobile legibility fails. The 740-unit SVG scales to roughly 315 px on a
   phone, putting the 15 px value labels at ~6.4 px rendered. The printed
   values are the chart's substitute for an axis, so when they become
   illegible the chart loses its scale entirely.
3. The gold used for small bold labels (#8a6410 on #f4ecdc) sits at roughly
   4.6:1 — passing, but with no margin at 10 px.
4. Vertical scanning in Act I (comparing Friday's visits, signups and rate)
   relies on eyeballing alignment across ~200 px of empty paper with no
   column guides.

CHANGES APPLIED (one pass)

- Score made a score: faint dashed measure lines now run through all three
  registers at every day boundary, extending the baselines to meet them.
  This develops the opera metaphor and directly fixes the vertical-scanning
  problem — one change serving both masters.
- Conversion named once, plainly: "S ÷ V" removed everywhere. The score's
  ratio register is labelled "SIGNUPS / PER VISIT" in the gutter, the ledger
  column reads "Per visit", and field No. 03 reads "Signups per visit". One
  quantity, one vocabulary.
- Curtain given cloth: subtle vertical fold shading over the gradient, so the
  proscenium band reads as drapery rather than a colored div.
- Act II retitled "The cast" — the playbill structure the table already had;
  the "Source" column header below it keeps literal meaning one line away.
- SVG type enlarged (values 15→19, days 12→14, ratios 13→17, register labels
  10→12) so phone-scale rendering stays readable; geometry rechecked for
  collisions.
- Label gold darkened to #78560c (~5.8:1) for the small bold labels; bright
  gold kept for bars and curtain lettering.

PRESERVED (strong decisions)

- The two-register daily score with printed values instead of axes; the
  ledger grid with proportional visit bars and printed figures; the
  "Synthetic Data" stamp as the data caveat in-identity; the overture
  fields with form numbering; the docket line; "The daily procession"; FIN.
- All figures, sums and ratios unchanged; no copy added beyond renamed
  labels and one act title.
