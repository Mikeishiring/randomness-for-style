Critique of 1-4-2.html (originality, then clarity), followed by the changes applied in 1-4-3.html.

ORIGINALITY — harsh
1. The fusion is still ornamental, not structural. V2 gave gold a grammar (gold = conversion), which was real progress, but the two ingredients otherwise live only in the frame: meander bands top and bottom, a laurel in the masthead, and nothing in between. Strip the header and footer and the body is a competent minimal report that could belong to any brand.
2. The laurel is decoration with no referent. In myth the laurel crowns a victor; here it floats under the title crowning nothing. Meanwhile the chart marks Sunday with a gold capital and gold numeral — a victor with no laurel. The page owns the right symbol and the right moment and fails to connect them.
3. Section heads ("Daily", "Sources") are generic template typography: caps, letterspacing, a hairline. The identity's strongest mark — the meander — never recurs at section level, so the middle of the page has no accent of its own.
4. The masthead is a centered stack straight from the luxury-brand template: over-line, big serif, ornament, tag. Defensible for a plaque, but composed with no risk whatsoever.
5. The "marble" is flat cream paint. A luxury-hotel surface trades on material; the backdrop has none.
6. The pillar fluting is effectively invisible at rendered size — a detail that exists mainly in the CSS, not on the page.

CLARITY — harsh
7. The peak marking is unexplained. Sunday's gold capital and gold numeral could read as emphasis, selection, or error; nothing states "best day of the week". The mark needs legible meaning, not just color.
8. The signups numeral row floats between the day labels and the conversion bars, anchored only by its left register label. Acceptable, but the weakest row in the ledger.
9. Bar scale maxima are unstated everywhere (pillars to 2,100; gold bars to 6.0%; table ink bars to 5,240). Defensible because every value is printed beside its mark, but a strict reader must infer the convention.
10. The sr-only table duplicates the chart for assistive tech while the visible chart is aria-hidden — workable, yet it means the page maintains two parallel truths that must never drift.

CHANGES APPLIED in 1-4-3.html
A. The laurel crowns the victor (answers 2, 7): small laurel sprigs now flank Sunday's visits numeral, repeating the masthead mark at the data. The gold capital stops being an arbitrary highlight: the same symbol introduced in the header names Sunday the week's best day, without a word of caption.
B. The meander recurs at section level (answers 3, and 1 in part): each section title carries a single small meander-spiral glyph, so the identity's signature appears where the information lives, not only on the frame.
C. Material backdrop (answers 5): the page backdrop gains a faint warm-and-ink tonal wash — kept outside the content panel, far below text contrast, deepening the surface without touching legibility.
D. Nothing else moved. Items 6, 8, 9 and 10 were weighed and left: removing the fluting deletes a harmless detail for no gain; re-anchoring the signups row would add rules or ink that the chart does not need; printing scale maxima would add numbers that duplicate what the printed values already state; collapsing the sr-only table into the visible grid would worsen the reading order for assistive tech.

PRESERVED (strong decisions)
The gold grammar from v2 (gold = conversion everywhere, one shared scale); pillar colonnade with capitals; conversion staircase demonstrating the 4.0% to 6.0% rise; signups preserved as numerals; meander bands; marble/ink/gold palette and type pairing; three-stat band; sources table with printed values; minimal copy; synthetic-data tag; sr-only data table. Purpose and data unchanged; every figure remains the supplied values or direct arithmetic on them.

NOT CHANGED, considered and rejected
A laurel on the pillar itself (clutter inside the chart body); a pediment-shaped masthead and asymmetric plaque composition (risk of regression for novelty's sake); narrative or mythic captions (copy competing with information); a totals row in the sources table (duplicates the stat band); renaming metrics to themed vocabulary (clarity cost).
