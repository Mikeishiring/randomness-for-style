Critique of 2-1-1.html (Weather + Disco weekly report)

ORIGINALITY — harsh read

1. The one genuinely merged idea — the disco ball standing in for the sun — is
   underbuilt. At 40px the blue sphere with a grid reads as a beach ball or a
   globe, not a mirror ball. No hanger stem, no mirrored facet glints, no
   scattered colored light. The single motif that carries the whole identity
   is not recognizable as itself.
2. Everything else disco is stock synthwave: pink/cyan/gold gradient title,
   glassy dark cards, glow shadows. That palette is the default "dark neon
   dashboard" of a thousand templates. Strip the three icons and nothing on
   the page says disco specifically — fail of the identity-removal test.
3. The weather half is borrowed app furniture used generically. The 7-day
   card strip is the weather-app cliche, and the page does not commit to the
   convention it borrowed: the rate plays the "temperature" role but nothing
   names the conditions. The metaphor ("overcast", "clear") exists only in
   aria-labels, so sighted readers never actually get the weather report —
   they get cards with an unexplained ball.
4. The background sparkle dots are decoration at homeopathic opacity; they
   neither commit to a texture nor earn their bytes.

CLARITY — harsh read

5. The legend is overloaded and does double duty badly. "bars: visits · ball:
   signup rate" is crammed in as a 'key' beside three thresholds; and the
   thresholds are only stated under the Daily strip while the Sources rows
   reuse the same icon scale a full section later, relying on memory.
6. In Sources, the bar encodes visits while the adjacent big bold number is
   the rate. Bar length and headline number encode different measures sitting
   millimetres apart; in this dataset they happen to rank the same way, which
   hides rather than removes the trap.
7. Sunday's gold "peak" border is an unexplained encoding. No key, no label.
   The tallest bar and the shine icon already mark the day; the border is a
   third, silent marker.
8. "signups" is spelled out seven times across the day cards — repetition
   doing the work a single column label should do.
9. The icon on day cards duplicates what the rate number says, which is fine
   as redundant encoding — but because the conditions are unnamed, the icon
   costs a legend lookup instead of paying for itself on sight.

WHAT STAYS (strong decisions preserved)

- Forecast-card structure with shared baseline bars: visits shape + per-day
  detail in one surface.
- Rate as the "temperature" of each day; icon as its condition.
- The same condition scale applied to sources, exposing Social's weak 3.1%.
- Totals row, synthetic-data badge, dark floor, tabular numerals, all data
  and derived figures unchanged.

REFINEMENTS APPLIED (one pass)

a. Rebuilt the ball as a mirror ball: hanger stem, silver-violet facet
   gradient, lit facet cells (white/cyan/pink), and for the top condition the
   uniform gold sun-rays became alternating pink/cyan/gold scattered light
   with a glint spark — disco and weather in one mark (addresses 1, 2).
b. Named the conditions on the day cards — OVERCAST / PARTLY LIT / FULL
   DISCO — tiny labels that make the weather report legible on sight and
   give the merged identity visible words, not just aria-text (3, 9).
c. Legend reduced to one job: the three condition icons with their signup-
   rate thresholds, still placed between the two sections it serves (5).
d. Sources rows regrouped by measure: name + visits bar with its counts
   directly beneath (volume), icon + bold % together at the right (rate), so
   bar length can no longer be misread as the rate (6).
e. Removed the unexplained Sunday border (7).
f. Units stated once, on the first entry of each series — "1,420 visits /
   57 signups" on Mon, "5,240 visits · 314 signups" on Direct — bare aligned
   numbers after (8).

Not changed: palette (kept but now anchored by a real mirror-ball motif),
layout order, chart geometry, all figures, self-containment.
