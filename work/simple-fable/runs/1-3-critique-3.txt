Critique of 1-3-3.html (harsh, originality and clarity), followed by the change applied
in 1-3-4.html.

ORIGINALITY

1. Both ingredients are now carried by drawn, specific elements rather than palette:
   myth by the Orphic lyre-in-wreath crest, the colonnade on a shared stylobate, the
   meander; opera by the gathered curtain with tassels, the staff with stemmed noteheads,
   the night numerals, the playbill header. Pushing further runs into kitsch, and every
   remaining idea on the table was already weighed and rejected for a clarity price:
   crescendo hairpins editorialize past the Thursday dip, act names displace day labels,
   mythic epithets obscure the only source names the owner uses, masks and string motifs
   in the table are noise. Originality criticism at this point would demand decoration,
   not design. No change.

2. The honest residual weakness is environmental, not compositional: on a stock Windows
   machine the Didot stack falls back to Times New Roman and the playbill voice dulls.
   Self-containment forbids shipping a font. Carried as a known constraint since pass one.

CLARITY

3. Geometry re-audited line by line: curtain fill and hem control points mirror exactly;
   stylobate top edge coincides with the zero line; every signup notehead, stem, label,
   and the melody polyline agree with y = 488 - (value - 40) x 1.1; column heights agree
   with 0.13 px per visit on a zero base. The Saturday note label (109) sits on the top
   staff line, which its dark halo was designed for. No numeric or positional defect found.

4. One real defect, and it has survived three passes unexamined: the mobile claim was
   never stress-tested. The chart is a single 920-unit-wide SVG scaled to container
   width; at a 375px phone the panel's inner width is about 315px, a 0.34 scale factor,
   so 12.5px labels render at roughly 4px. Every value, axis figure, day name, and
   conversion percentage becomes illegible precisely on the device where a site owner
   checks weekly numbers. The sources table solved the same problem with a horizontal
   scroll container from the first version; the chart inconsistently never received the
   same treatment. That inconsistency is the justified change.

5. Considered and rejected for this fix: a stacked mobile re-layout of the chart
   (a structural change far beyond "change only what improves it", with new collision
   risks across all seven groups), and shrinking chart margins per media query (SVG
   internals do not respond to CSS media queries without scripting, and scripting is
   worth avoiding in a page whose integrity rests on being static). The scroll container
   matches the table's existing behavior, costs nothing on desktop, and guarantees a
   minimum text size. Tradeoff accepted and shared with every scrolling table: the
   y-axis pans out of view while scrolled — mitigated by the value labels carried on
   every column and note.

CHANGE APPLIED IN 1-3-4.html

a. The daily chart is wrapped in the same .scroll container the table uses, and .chart
   gains min-width:680px. On desktop nothing moves; below ~740px viewports the chart
   scrolls horizontally instead of shrinking its text past legibility (minimum effective
   label size about 9px).

Nothing else was touched: data, scales, figures, labels, tooltips, crest, curtain,
stylobate, table, and all text are identical to 1-3-3.html. This is the point of
diminishing returns; absent new requirements, a further pass would be change for its
own sake.
