CRITIQUE OF 3-2-2.html

ORIGINALITY — harsh read

1. The fusion now exists, but only in the header. The stamped receipt is
   the one object on the page where bureaucracy and fast food actually
   meet. Below the fold the page reverts to a competent official-form
   pastiche that any "government form" brief would produce: the chart and
   the ledger carry the palette, but nothing in their construction owes
   anything to either ingredient. The identity is front-loaded.

2. The three CONV boxes are still identical triplets. Round one called
   this out — a stamp repeated with machine precision reads as a styled
   component, not a stamp — and round one's revision did not fix it. A
   rubber stamp's whole character is that no two strikes land the same.

3. The chart's axis stub is the thinnest possible gesture at a printed
   form: a bold word and a min–max range in 9px. Real forms and real
   graph paper rule the field. The band behind the bars is blank manila,
   which is a missed opportunity for the single most bureaucratic texture
   available: ruled grid.

4. The staggered signage bars (red bar, then indented mustard bar) sit
   between two stools — not aligned like a form, not composed like a
   sign. Considered for change; kept, because every alternative tried on
   paper (flush-left stack, centered stack) was blander and the stagger
   does echo stacked diner signage. Noted as taste, not strength.

CLARITY — harsh read

1. "0–2,100" is a range note, not a scale. To judge Wednesday's 1,780
   against the band the reader must read every printed number; the chart
   itself offers no reference line between zero and the top. Same for the
   signups band. A chart whose only calibration is two endpoints printed
   in 9px mute type is calibrated in name only.

2. The gap left when short signup bars hang far above their aligned
   number row (Mon: 22px bar, then empty space, then "57") is tolerable
   because the rows scan as a table, but unruled empty space between bar
   and value makes the eye trace nothing. Rules would carry the eye.

3. Minor: the empty spacer row in the axis column (opposite the signup
   numbers) is inert; acceptable, since labeling it would duplicate
   "SIGNUPS".

4. Otherwise the round-one fixes hold: both bands declare their own
   scales, conversion is anchored to a named CONV % row, the peak marker
   no longer borrows the signups color, value rows align.

WHAT IS STRONG AND KEPT

The stamped receipt with conversion as the receipt total; the two-ink
signage title in the form's own Courier; the awning; mustard=visits /
red=signups; a printed number on every bar; aligned value rows under the
baseline; item letters 2A–2C; peak day named in the receipt and marked in
ink; all figures unchanged; fully static and self-contained.

CHANGES APPLIED IN 3-2-3.html

1. Ruled the chart like graph paper. Both bands get faint horizontal
   rules printed behind the bars — at 500/1,000/1,500/2,000 visits and at
   50/100 signups — with small tick labels in the axis column replacing
   the min–max range notes. One move serves both masters: it is the
   printed-form grid the bureaucracy was missing, and it turns the chart
   from endpoint-calibrated to genuinely readable at intermediate values.
2. Gave each CONV stamp its own slight rotation (-2, +1.5, -1.2 degrees)
   so the three strikes read as stamped by hand rather than templated.
   Legibility is untouched at these angles.
3. Nothing else changed. Renaming sources as menu items ("COMBO 2A") was
   rejected as flavor that destroys plainness; receipt header chrome
   ("ORDER #") was rejected as duplicating the form tag; ink hatching on
   bars was rejected as texture that costs contrast and adds nothing.

Purpose, data, structure, bar proportions, and all computed figures are
unchanged from 3-2-2.html.
