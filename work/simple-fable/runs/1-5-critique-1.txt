CRITIQUE OF 1-5-1.html

ORIGINALITY — harsh reading

1. The Greek ingredient is skin-deep. It amounts to one meander strip at the top
   and three italic binomials. The meander is the single most predictable Greek
   signifier available, and it is applied as a detached decorative band with no
   relationship to anything below it. Strip the band and the three names and the
   page is a generic "old paper" dashboard. Nothing Greek structures the page or
   touches the data.

2. The Victorian ingredient stops at quotation. Insect plates, double-rule
   museum-label frames, letterspaced caps: all correct, none surprising. The
   daily chart's "pins" read as an ordinary lollipop chart; the entomological
   conceit is invisible unless you are told about it.

3. The two ingredients fuse exactly once — Athena's moth carries owl eyespots,
   and the binomials encode their source's behaviour (Hermes directus, Athena
   quaerens, Echo gregaria). That is the best idea on the page and it is nearly
   invisible. Psyche, the obvious myth-insect bridge, goes unused; Greek pottery,
   the obvious visual vocabulary, goes unused.

4. Typography is default Georgia with tracking. Serviceable, not distinctive.

CLARITY — harsh reading

5. The conversion row is orphaned. Day labels sit between the visits and signups
   bands, so Sunday's 6.0% is roughly 170px below its day label with no column
   guide. Tracing a column across that gap is real work. Worst defect on the page.

6. Conversion is never defined. "Signups ÷ visits" is standard, but the page
   makes the reader supply the relationship between its three rows.

7. In the source cards, each metric's bar is scaled to the leading source for
   that metric. The convention is honest but unstated, and within a card it
   invites a false reading: Direct shows equal full-length bars for 5,240 visits
   and a 6.0% rate. Values are labeled, which limits the damage, but the scale
   should be declared once.

8. Accepted tradeoffs, defended: the chart scrolls horizontally on phones rather
   than shrinking below legibility; marks are uniform with no peak-day
   highlighting, because every value is labeled and Sunday's lead is visible
   without editorial color.

CHANGES APPLIED

- Daily chart regrouped: both count bands stack first, then day labels with the
  conversion percentages directly beneath them. Day and rate now read as one
  specimen label; the orphan is gone. (Fixes 5.)
- Conversion row labeled "signups ÷ visits" at the row head, defining the KPI
  and the card rows in one place. (Fixes 6.)
- One consolidated scale note under Sources: "Bars scaled to the leading
  source." Covers all nine bars without per-card copy. (Fixes 7.)
- Greek pottery joined to Victorian mounting: a faint terracotta amphora
  silhouette now sits behind each pinned specimen, so every source plate fuses
  both ingredients in the artifact itself rather than in a border. (Develops 1,
  3; decorative, 13% opacity, aria-hidden, no clarity cost.)
- The meander is no longer one detached strip: section rules under "Sources"
  now use a muted key pattern, accent key at the head. The motif recurs through
  the page instead of captioning it. (Develops 1.)

PRESERVED DELIBERATELY

- Two separately scaled count bands instead of a dual-axis chart; every value
  labeled; derived conversions shown as rates, not bars mixed with counts.
- The myth binomials and owl eyespots — the one working fusion, now supported
  rather than replaced.
- Synthetic-data tag beside the title; museum-label frames; palette and dark
  theme; aria-hidden plates with text names carrying meaning.
- All figures unchanged and drawn only from the supplied data.
