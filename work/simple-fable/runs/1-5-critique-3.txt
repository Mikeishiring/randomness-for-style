CRITIQUE OF 1-5-3.html

ORIGINALITY — harsh reading

1. The identity has stopped developing and started consolidating. That is
   partly right — a weekly report must be boring to re-read — but the honest
   account is that three zones remain generically styled and no safe
   development was found for them, again: the title block and KPI row are
   plain letterspaced Georgia; the daily chart marks are neutral lollipops;
   the amphora ghosts hover at the floor of legibility behind the larger
   insects. Each was weighed in earlier passes against concrete alternatives
   (ornamented KPI labels, figurative marks, per-source vessels) and each
   alternative loses more clarity than it gains identity. Those rejections
   still hold. The design is now conservative by argument rather than by
   neglect, but a harsher judge would say the experiment's ceiling on
   originality was set in pass one and never raised: the fusion lives in the
   source plates and the frame, and nowhere else.

2. One frame defect is real: the meander opens the page in accent and recurs
   at the section rule, but the page just stops after the cards. A Victorian
   plate closes its frame. The cabinet has a lid and no floor.

CLARITY — harsh reading

3. Measured, not eyeballed: the muted sepia #7a6a52 reaches only 4.34:1
   against the paper background — below WCAG AA (4.5:1) for the small tag,
   note, and section heading that sit directly on paper, and a bare 4.61:1 on
   the cards. Small-cap labels at 10–11px are precisely where contrast
   shortfalls hurt.

4. Sunday's signups value "127" sits 3px above the 140 gridline, which clips
   the bottom of its bounding box. Cosmetic, but it is the biggest number in
   the band and the one most likely to be read.

5. Otherwise the chart now reads cleanly: day labels and conversion sit
   between the bands, every series is within ~60px of its day label, scales
   are separate and honest, every value is labeled, the bar-scale note is
   consolidated. No structural clarity defect found.
