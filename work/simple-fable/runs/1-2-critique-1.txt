CRITIQUE — run 1-2-1 (Agora Grill, Week 01)

ORIGINALITY — harsh read

1. The "Greek myth" ingredient is mostly Greece-the-country, not myth. A meander
   frieze, Anthora blue-and-cream, a temple logo: that is the New York diner cup,
   a borrowed surface. The only actual mythology on the page is three name chips
   (Hermes, The Oracle, The Sirens), and they do no work — strip them and nothing
   about the report changes. Myth never carries meaning anywhere.
2. The fast-food ingredient is thinner still: a burger in the logo and big Impact
   numbers "like a menu board". Nothing actually reads as a menu, a ticket, or a
   price. "The Week's Order" promises a menu metaphor the tiles never deliver.
3. The two ingredients never combine. Greek frieze, then a generic KPI grid, then
   a generic bar chart, then a generic stat table. It is a tasteful neo-brutalist
   dashboard wearing two stickers — current-trend styling (hard shadows, pills,
   rounded cards), not a distinct identity.
4. Impact everywhere is a shortcut. One voice does both gods and burgers, so
   neither ingredient gets a typographic voice of its own.

CLARITY — harsh read

5. The gold "signups within visits" base segment is 6-13px tall: nearly invisible,
   easily misread as a baseline highlight, and the legend phrase "Signups, within
   visits" is cryptic. (Its tininess is honest — conversion IS ~5% — but it needs
   a crisp edge and plainer words.)
6. Best-day tile: giant "Sun" plus the unlabeled triple "2,100 · 127 · 6.0%" makes
   the reader guess what each number is. The 6.0% also duplicates the conversion
   tile's trend endpoint.
7. Sources: "Visits · 42% of all" crams two facts into one 10px label. Worse, the
   visits bar and signups bar sit side by side at the SAME height on DIFFERENT
   scales (each normalized to its own column max), inviting a length comparison
   that means nothing. The printed numbers rescue it; the geometry shouldn't need
   rescuing.
8. The epithet chips are styled exactly like interactive filter pills (border,
   pill radius, uppercase). They are decoration pretending to be controls.
9. The signups tile's "peak 127 on Sun" duplicates the best-day tile.

PRESERVED (strong decisions, kept deliberately)

- Overall structure: totals -> daily -> sources; all supplied figures and all
  derived figures unchanged.
- Myth-title + literal-subtitle double labeling of sections (identity without
  hiding what a section is).
- Values printed on every bar; full numeric table under the chart; honest
  same-scale signups segment rather than a misleading dual axis.
- Meander frieze, palette, logo, synthetic-data tag, card system.

CHANGES APPLIED IN 1-2-2 (one pass, each justified)

a. Myth gets a voice and a job: epithets become small serif-italic lines that
   explain the god-to-channel mapping ("Hermes · the straight road", "the Oracle ·
   asked and answered", "the Sirens · the distant song") and lose the fake-control
   pill styling. Serif italic = myth voice; Impact caps stays the menu voice. (1,4,8)
b. Sunday's bar is crowned with a small gold star marking the peak — the week
   reads as a climb to its summit, and the maximum is now marked in the chart
   itself, not only in the table tint. (1,2 restrained)
c. Chart legend reworded to "Signups — the gold base of each bar"; gold segments
   get a crisp paper-colored edge so they read as a segment, not a glow. (5)
d. Best-day tile: value "Sunday", sub-line labeled "2,100 visits · 127 signups";
   the duplicate 6.0% dropped. Signups tile sub-line becomes "+123% Mon → Sun",
   parallel to the visits tile and no longer duplicating the best-day tile. (6,9)
e. Sources: share moved out of the label into a muted suffix after the number
   ("5,240 · 42% of all"); signups bars thinned to 6px so the two bar columns no
   longer present as comparable lengths on one scale. (7)
f. Receipt detail: the sources total row gets a double rule, the accounting/
   receipt convention for a total — a fast-food cue that is also a reading cue. (2)

Not changed: no new sections, no new copy beyond the three epithets, no layout
reshuffle — the dashboard was clear; it needed its identity to work harder, not
more furniture.
