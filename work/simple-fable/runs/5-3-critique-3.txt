Critique of 5-3-3.html

ORIGINALITY

1. Converged but conservative: v3 repaired mechanics and invented nothing.
   Three passes in, the whole invention budget is still the orb row, one
   beam and a curtain edge. Everything else is a competent dark report.

2. The strongest fusion on the page was never designed on purpose: the gold
   baseline reads as a stage edge, with the glowing gold "pit" of orbs below
   the columns — an orchestra pit under the stage. It is latent and
   unclaimed. (It should also stay implicit: captioning a metaphor would
   cost more than it pays, so this is noted as found value, not a change.)

3. Act II is still the least in-world region: a competent table whose
   identity is inherited colour, not an idea. The header meanwhile is pure
   opera and the chart mostly pure sea — the two ingredients alternate by
   section more than they merge within one.

4. The playbill header formula (small-caps overline, italic Didone title,
   double rules) is handsome and also the most predictable possible
   rendering of "opera", done perfectly straight.

CLARITY

5. The per-day conversion figures — the single most decision-relevant row on
   the page, since they carry the 4.0% → 6.0% crescendo — are set at the
   smallest size and lowest contrast in the chart (11.5px, #a9cad2), falling
   below 10px effective on tablet widths. The hierarchy punishes the best
   content.

6. The sources block is a table in content but divs in semantics: a screen
   reader hears "Visits Signups Conversion" once, then three unlabeled
   triplets of numbers. No header-cell association, and the Source column
   has no header at all.

7. The decorative fleurons (black diamonds) are pseudo-element content and
   are announced by screen readers as "black diamond" noise either side of
   the overline.

8. Accepted limitation, not fixed: the chart's left captions scroll away
   with the horizontal scroller on phones — the cost of keeping the chart a
   single scalable SVG with no scripts.

CHANGES APPLIED IN 5-3-4.html

a. The conversion row is raised to 12.5px and brightened (#b9d6dd); the
   left captions go to 11.5px. The crescendo is no longer the faintest
   thing on the chart.
b. The sources block gains table semantics via ARIA roles (table, row,
   rowheader, columnheader, cell) plus a visually hidden "Source" column
   header, so rows read as labelled records.
c. The fleurons are hidden from assistive tech using the content
   alternative-text syntax, with a plain-content fallback so older browsers
   still render them.
d. Nothing new is ornamented. The stage-edge/orchestra-pit reading stays
   implicit, and no visual element moves.

PRESERVED (strong decisions): all geometry, scales and encodings, the beam,
curtain, playbill header, KPI triad, per-day conversion data, printed values,
copy level, and self-containment. This pass is deliberately small: the two
ingredients are developed enough that further additions would start taxing
the report's readability, which the brief forbids.
