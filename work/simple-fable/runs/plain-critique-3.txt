Critique of plain-3.html

ORIGINALITY — one gesture is not an identity.
1. The ledger voice rests entirely on the double masthead rule; below it, the
   page relaxes back into competent anonymity. Section heads are bare gray
   small-caps floating in whitespace — they belong to any minimal template and
   do not participate in the page's rule system. The ledger device (rules that
   structure and recur) is stated once and never developed.
2. The marks themselves are generic: plain bars, plain dots, plain table. The
   one place the data invites a distinctive mark — the conversion trend — is
   rendered as the most inert option available.

CLARITY — the conversion row underperforms its own data.
3. Conversion is an ordered time series, and its story (steady climb, Thursday
   dip, strong weekend finish) lives in the dot-to-dot differences — which are
   3 to 4 pixels, read across seven separated cells. Disconnected dots force
   the reader to reconstruct the line mentally; the series earns an actual
   connecting line, and the labels already protect against overreading it.
4. Non-visual readers get three unlabeled sequences of seven numbers each;
   the day names arrive only afterward, in a separate row, so "1,420" has no
   programmatic day. Association by visual column position is no association
   at all for a screen reader.
5. Minor: the 10px grid gaps mean columns are not exact sevenths of the row,
   which blocks any overlay mark (like a trend line) from aligning with the
   columns. The gaps buy nothing — bars are already narrower than their cells.

STRONG DECISIONS — preserved unchanged.
- KPI band, double rule, serif numerals, paper/ink palette, green=visits /
  ochre=rate coding, shape-coded keys, bar-attached labels on exact pixel
  scales, continuous baselines, bare comparative bars in the source table,
  tabular numerals, minimal copy, dark mode, self-containment. All figures
  and arithmetic identical.

CHANGES MADE in plain-4.html
a. The conversion dots are now connected by a thin line (inline SVG, marked
   decorative, non-scaling stroke) on the same zero-based scale; the dots and
   per-day % labels stay, so no detail is lost and the trend becomes legible.
b. Day-section grid gaps removed so every column is an exact seventh; the
   line's vertices sit precisely on column centers. Bar widths are unchanged
   (already capped at 42px).
c. Section heads now extend into a hairline rule across the page — the ledger
   rule system recurs at every level (double at masthead, ruled heads, hairline
   baselines) instead of appearing once.
d. Each daily value gains a visually hidden day prefix (e.g. "Mon 1,420") and
   the visual day-axis row is marked aria-hidden, so screen readers hear each
   number with its day exactly once.
No content, figure, or derived value changed; purpose and data unchanged.
