The previous revision is clearer, but calling two ordinary tables drawers does not make them a collecting cabinet. Both sections have identical visual rhythm even though one is a chronological record and the other a classification. The repeated row numbers add administrative theater without aiding recognition. The oversized beetle plate still concentrates nearly all the insect-collecting identity in the header. The tiny source bars also conceal the most useful source comparison: conversion quality.

Keep the daily register, where a ruled chronological table earns its place. Replace the second table with one divided specimen tray: three adjoining compartments, one for each source. Each compartment has an italic serif identification label, visits and signups on aligned rows, and a large, directly comparable signup rate. This makes classification a spatial organizing principle. The source rates gain a common zero-to-7% scale with an explicitly labeled 5.00% weekly benchmark; the visual now supports the source finding rather than merely echoing traffic counts. Use one monochrome pin-like benchmark line across those three charts, with no decorative insects added.

Remove redundant daily accession numbers and replace the dark numbered section blocks with inset catalog labels on the cabinet rule. Reduce the header specimen footprint and make Website performance the primary heading so the purpose is immediate. Preserve the synthetic period, all supplied figures, all rates, the daily count bars, and the prior-period limitation. The source tray stacks on small screens, where all values remain visible. This is a single cabinet-and-register idea with two meaningful arrangements, not an accumulation of antique motifs.
