The existing page is a conventional analytics dashboard wearing a striped hat. The awning, mustard badge and invented department name sit around a familiar three-card summary; they do not determine how the figures are read. The rotated week label is decorative paperwork rather than useful bureaucracy. Daily signups are detached from their days in a second grid, with a sentence explaining how to decode them. The source bars add another chart without a visible scale.

Use one idea: a weekly service return printed at a fast-food counter. A compact red menu board carries the three unchanged metric labels and totals; beneath it, an itemized ruled return puts each day's visits, signups and rate in one readable row. The visits bars share a zero baseline and the same width scale, while exact figures remain visible. Source totals become a second register in the same form. Heavy condensed headings, tabular figures, red rules and receipt paper do the identity work; remove the awning, rotated badge, generic metric cards and unexplained source bars. Keep the two useful observations, all supplied counts, calculated rates and one consolidated synthetic-data note. On narrow screens the menu totals stack and the register remains a four-column table.
