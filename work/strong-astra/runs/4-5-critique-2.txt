The first revision claims a stronger identity than it actually delivers. Its cabinet is useful, but the design could still be an archive of anything: there is no identifiable insect, and the oversized week rectangle is merely a generic racing-number badge. The thin stalks on the bars do not communicate insect collecting without an explanation. They also extend beyond the measured bar and make the upper endpoint unnecessarily ambiguous. The chart rules are arbitrary, unlabelled intervals. Finally, the side-by-side day names and numbers are cramped in seven desktop compartments, especially Wednesday. Calling this a finished timing cabinet was premature.

This pass sharpens the same idea rather than inventing another layout. The rectangular week badge is replaced by one original pinned beetle engraving whose wing cases carry the week number as a racing roundel. The insect and the racing identifier are now one object, not adjacent logos. The cabinet structure, italic collector labels, racing numerals and monospaced measurement slips continue through the data. Daily number plates now sit above full day names, giving the typography a deliberate hierarchy and sufficient width. The faux pins disappear from the quantitative bars; their function is taken by the actual specimen in the masthead. Bar tops are explicit measurement caps, and the shared chart rules now correspond to labelled 1,000-visit intervals. Sunday retains a restrained double finish rule without acquiring a second metric colour. All totals, daily values, source values, rates and substantive findings are preserved. No extra card, legend of theme metaphors, decorative footer, external asset or invented data is added.
