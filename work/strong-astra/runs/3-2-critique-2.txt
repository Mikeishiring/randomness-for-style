The first revision still reads as a normal full-width dashboard: a horizontal KPI strip, yellow announcement band, then two tables. Its claims to be a menu board and a receipt mostly live in the colors. The numbered boxes are badges with little purpose. Worse, the visit figures sit over the bar ends, hiding the very length differences the chart is meant to reveal.

Commit to a counter and its itemized return. Put the weekly headline figures on one tall, red menu panel, with the reporting-period field and the Sunday observation belonging to that same panel. Give the right-hand paper register uninterrupted space for daily and source records. This makes the fast-food hierarchy and bureaucratic tabulation determine the actual layout, rather than decorating a dashboard template. Remove the section-number boxes and use plain register numbers. Replace the covered bars with unobstructed thin bars below their exact visit counts, preserving a common zero baseline and scale. Keep every count, rate, metric label and source statement. At smaller widths, the counter becomes a compact heading and three label-value rows above the same readable tables; no detail is discarded.
