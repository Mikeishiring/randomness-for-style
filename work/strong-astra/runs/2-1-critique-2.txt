The first revision is more coherent, but its claim to combine Weather and Disco is still too generous. A seven-column layout alone does not evoke weather; it is simply the obvious way to chart a week. Acid-yellow equalizer bars read as a music dashboard, while the cream header and condensed headline could belong to a sports publication. The weather ingredient is mostly confined to “brighter.” Its most distinctive graphic speaks only one half of the brief. The generic source bars also abandon the visual language as soon as the main panel ends.

Replace the equalizer with one shared image: a mirrored sun rising above the horizon. Each day's mirror-disc sits at its actual observed value on a zero-based scale; the connecting contour follows the supplied daily figures. A faint vertical beam anchors each observation to zero. This is a historical chart, not invented weather, and neither the title nor labels describe the data as a forecast. Forecast-strip day labels move above the observations, while exact figures remain aligned below. A warmer sunlight-and-midnight palette replaces the acidic club palette. The same square segmentation quietly carries into source bars, so the Disco ingredient remains part of quantitative presentation rather than becoming a standalone badge.

The metaphor now depends on one useful graphic instead of multiple decorative motifs. Retain the metric switch, weekly totals, all daily values and rates, source counts and rates, keyboard access and the compact calculation note. Clarify the page's website-performance purpose in the header. Give the Sunday finding a concise observation-style treatment instead of another generic sentence. No unsupported comparison or new data is added.
