The existing page has accurate totals and useful daily detail, but the theme lives mainly in two burger illustrations and playful headings. The rounded KPI strip, neutral chart, and generic takeaway section could belong to almost any dashboard. “Forecast” also blurs the distinction between observed traffic and a prediction. The oversized introductory claim repeats Sunday’s highlight later, while separate tables make the day-to-metric relationship weaker than it needs to be.

Replace that structure with one coherent idea: a roadside diner’s weather-and-orders board. A dark menu-board frame holds the weekly totals and a seven-day weather-strip layout; each day has its actual visits, signups, and signup rate in one accessible table. Warm cream, tomato red, condensed uppercase headings, and tabular receipt type carry the identity without added illustrations. The traffic-source section becomes the attached order ticket, with aligned columns and proportional visit bars. Keep the observations explicitly retrospective, retain every supplied figure and the rate calculation, and consolidate interpretation into one source comparison. On narrow screens the daily board scrolls as one labelled region rather than compressing numbers beyond readability.
