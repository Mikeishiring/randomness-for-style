The original is a competent generic dashboard with a costume: two tiled circles supply nearly all the Weather/Disco identity. Remove them and its serif headline, rounded KPI card, blue bars and stacked panels could describe almost any analytics product. “Daily rhythm” does no visual work. The purple Sunday bar is arbitrary, and the chart's unlabeled horizontal rules suggest a scale without supplying one. Its intro and panel notes repeat findings already visible in the figures, while the large daily table repeats the entire chart.

One stronger idea: a weather-station board built like a disco equalizer. Seven forecast-style columns become the main organizing device, with mirrored, segmented bars above a compact daily readout. Weather contributes the seven-day strip, horizon and observation-board hierarchy; Disco contributes the square mirror grid, condensed uppercase display lettering and illuminated equalizer bars. Both operate on the actual data presentation instead of sitting beside it as icons. A cream, midnight-blue and acid-yellow palette makes the chart the focal point without assigning arbitrary colors to days.

Replace the detached decorative marks, generic rounded metric card and redundant daily table with one integrated weekly board. Keep all daily figures and rates directly under their corresponding bars, retain the Visits/Signups control, add a labeled zero-based scale, and preserve every source figure and signup rate in a compact comparison table. The source bars share the same scale. Keep the headline finding but cut its repetitive paragraph and other obvious explanatory copy. The period and synthetic-data label appear together once; rate calculation remains beside the tables. No comparison period or forecast is invented.
