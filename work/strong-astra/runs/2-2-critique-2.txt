The previous version moved beyond generic cards, but its strongest identity was almost entirely diner: a dark menu, a receipt, and condensed type. Weather remained a logo and the word “outlook.” That word was also wrong for recorded performance. “Biggest orders” implied an order or revenue metric that the data does not contain. The isolated daily bars and large prose block made the result feel like a dashboard dressed as a restaurant.

The revised page commits to a diner’s weekly weather bulletin. A tomato-red board header works as the menu sign, while the seven-day strip now uses one continuous, zero-based visits profile with aligned observations beneath it, borrowing the presentation of a weather record without inventing temperatures or conditions. Exact visits, signups, and signup rates remain in the same semantic table; Sunday’s highlighted column identifies the weekly high. The source receipt keeps all three metrics, source comparisons, and totals. Its typography now resembles an actual printed ticket instead of repeating the menu heading. Replace the misleading “outlook” and “orders” language with “record” and “Direct leads the week.” Keep the combined sun-and-burger mark as the sole illustration. No new metrics, controls, decorative badges, or supporting sections are needed.
