The shared mirror-sun chart is finally a real visual idea. Replacing it again would be novelty for its own sake. The remaining weakness is the surrounding hierarchy: the enormous condensed all-caps headline still belongs to a sports poster, and it forces an otherwise compact report into a stacked hero, totals band, chart and source table. The Sunday callout adds a competing typographic voice. The segmented source bars are also thematic overreach: they resemble discrete counts without representing any unit. The yellow signup-rate row grants that metric a special emphasis unrelated to the selected chart metric.

Keep the mirrored suns and weather-strip observations as the central identity. Replace the billboard typography with a compact, warm serif headline and a single italic “brighter,” allowing the retro Disco influence to soften the weather-station numerals without adding ornament. Put the finding and weekly totals beside one another on wide screens, bringing the useful chart forward; stack them naturally on narrower screens. Use the same restrained serif for section titles and retain monospace for observations. Remove the standalone Sunday display treatment, arbitrary rate-row highlighting and decorative source segmentation. Continuous source bars are easier to interpret and allow the distinctive main chart to remain distinctive.

Consolidate the accumulated CSS into the final design rather than retaining successive overrides. Preserve all supplied figures, derived rates, table semantics, metric selection and keyboard scrolling. Add a no-script fallback that reveals the daily table's day headings and removes the inactive control, so the complete report remains understandable even without its chart script. No added figures, implied forecast or comparison period.
