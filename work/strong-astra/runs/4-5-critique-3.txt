The beetle roundel now fuses the two ingredients convincingly, but the information layout still behaves like a generic card grid. Seven equal specimen boxes repeat the same three labels and spend most of their height on narrow bars. At tablet and phone widths the week fractures into several rows with different baselines; comparing Monday with Sunday requires scrolling and remembering. The three source cards repeat the same mistake in a second format. The collector metaphor has become an excuse for containers, rather than a useful way to order evidence. Adding more insects would not solve this.

This revision replaces the card grid with one ruled timing register, arranged like a collector's accession ledger. Numbered entries, italic specimen names and fixed measurement columns are the shared visual grammar for both daily and source data. Racing classification shapes the row order, numeric typography and double finish rule; collecting shapes the paired name-and-number labels, paper register and engraved week specimen. The beetle roundel remains the sole illustration. Visits become horizontal bars within the visits column, so every day retains an aligned zero baseline on every screen. Values are visible alongside the bars; signups and conversion occupy stable comparison columns. The source collection uses the identical structure rather than a separate card language. Small screens use familiar day abbreviations and tighter spacing without dropping any metric or removing the chart. This removes repeated labels and large empty compartments, preserves all supplied figures and findings, and makes the identity more consistent by replacing the weakest structure rather than decorating it.
