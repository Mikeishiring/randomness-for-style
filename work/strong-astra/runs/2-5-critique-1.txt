The original is a conventional analytics dashboard wearing a natural-history badge. The butterfly emblem and separate illustrated note are removable decorations; neither changes how the information is organised. “The daily weather” is merely a themed heading above ordinary bars. The source list is a generic set of progress tracks. A large serif title and cream background supply period flavour, but not the specific character of a Victorian collector's meteorological record. Repeated weekly context, definitions and footer attribution add copy without making the data easier to inspect.

The revision commits to a single object: an observatory's specimen register. A ruled weather trace and its daily readings occupy the main sheet; three attached collection drawers organise the traffic sources. The shared numbered compartments, fine brass rules, specimen silhouettes, serif labels and monospaced measurements give both sections one visual grammar. Weather now shapes the main graph and register layout, while collecting shapes the source grouping and taxonomy. The ornamental explanatory plate is removed; its useful limitation becomes one short note. The headline metrics retain their literal labels, every supplied figure remains visible, and conversion and visit-share calculations retain their original precision. The chart starts at zero, and the full table remains the exact-value alternative. The source bars use a common 100% scale. No historical weather, benchmark, causal explanation or comparison period is invented.
