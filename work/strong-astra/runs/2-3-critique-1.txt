The original is a conventional dashboard wearing a cloud logo. Its rounded chart roof is the only theatrical spatial gesture; the weather language lives almost entirely in “outlook.” Georgia headings, three summary cards and a two-column lower section could belong to any template. The Sunday accent is arbitrary, and the decorative rings contribute nothing to reading the data. More seriously, the flex-based bars can shrink to accommodate their labels, weakening the claimed shared scale.

The revision commits to one idea: a weather observation bulletin printed as an opera programme. A narrow typographic billing column holds the weekly figures; the main performance occupies a large proscenium-shaped plotting field. Theatre red defines the programme structure and weather blue consistently denotes visits. The chart uses fixed SVG coordinates, a visible zero baseline and ruled 1,000-visit intervals, so its presentation is also a measurement instrument. The serif display typography, numbered programme sections and restrained double rules carry the opera identity without additional illustrations. Daily and source details remain explicit and complete, with conversion definitions and synthetic provenance consolidated. Redundant metric captions and the generic introductory paragraph are removed. The layout stacks on small screens, where the chart retains its scale through horizontal scrolling rather than shrinking its labels beyond legibility.
