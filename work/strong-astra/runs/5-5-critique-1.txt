The original page is a conventional dashboard wearing a dark teal palette. Its beetle is a detachable logo; the Roman numerals and serif headings do little to make Victorian collecting shape the information. Deep-sea bioluminescence is reduced to a gradient repeated on ordinary bars. The generic three-metric strip, floating source card and full-width table do not belong to one convincing visual system. Unlabelled chart grid lines add the appearance of measurement without a readable scale. Percentage-height bars can also shrink under their labels on small screens, compromising the comparison.

The revision commits to a single idea: an illuminated entomological specimen drawer. A brass-edged dark case contains a paper catalogue strip, a daily specimen tray and a source tray. Daily visit bars become precisely scaled luminous specimen pins over a shared zero baseline; their paper tickets carry every day's visits, signups and rate, replacing the disconnected duplicate ledger. The source tray uses the same pin-and-ticket language with a common visits scale. Restrained insect anatomy lives in the case's title plate, while serif catalogue typography, ruled compartments and aligned numerical columns carry the collecting identity throughout. Light is confined to the data marks, so the abyssal palette serves comparison instead of atmosphere alone. All supplied figures and clear metric labels remain; derived rates and totals are unchanged. On narrow screens the daily tray becomes a fully labelled horizontal chart with the same exact relative lengths, preserving legibility without shrinking or scrolling the data.
