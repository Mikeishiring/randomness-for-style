The service-window composition now has a credible idea, but two decisions still look borrowed rather than designed. The numbered section labels pretend the page is a menu without giving the numbers any meaning. They are decoration disguised as information. More significantly, the source section is still a generic table: the useful relationship between 5,240, 4,480 and 2,760 visits has to be calculated mentally, while the daily section benefits from luminous comparison marks. The receipt repeats an obvious seven-day count. On intermediate widths, the narrow receipt rail also squeezes the large totals too far; preserving the metaphor is not a reason to compress the information.

The revision keeps one idea rather than inventing another theme layer. It removes the meaningless section numbers and the redundant day count. Source visit counts now carry short luminous measurement bars within the Visits column, making the same shared-scale light language work across both the daily chart and the source menu. The numbers remain explicit, the table headers remain literal, and all three bars use the same width scale with a zero origin. The bars carry no new data and are hidden from assistive technology because their exact values are already in the cells. Dotted table rules and condensed menu typography remain the fast-food structure; concentrated light against deep dark surfaces remains the bioluminescent structure. The receipt moves below the board at 900px so neither the numerical hierarchy nor the tabular detail needs to fight for width. All figures, calculated rates, supported findings and source context are preserved. Further decorative ocean imagery would weaken this useful, coherent composition.
