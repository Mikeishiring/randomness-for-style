The first refinement has a coherent fast-food menu and receipt, but the Greek ingredient is still more architecture than myth. A triangular roof and a lone lightning character can pass for a generic heritage restaurant. The section numbers are inert dashboard furniture; they do not help the owner compare performance. The unmodulated sans-serif wordmark also weakens the inscription-like title, while the chart ignores the architectural structure that surrounds it.

This pass makes the menu board itself an Olympian temple. An original, compact line drawing of Zeus, his thunderbolt and a burger replaces the ambiguous bolt in the pediment; it is the one identifying illustration, rather than another decorative layer. Inscription-style Olympus lettering pairs with a compact drive-in label. Fluted visit bars form a colonnade while preserving their zero baseline, proportional heights and exact labels. Source names adopt the menu's serif type, and the daily receipt gains a simple printed rule. Arbitrary section numbers are removed. Clear metric labels, every supplied daily and source count, conversion rates, useful findings and the single provenance note are retained. The receipt still stacks below the board on small screens.
