The second revision is more coherent, but its specimen cabinet still makes an arbitrary association between Direct, Search and Social and three unrelated insects. Those drawings act as oversized category icons, not collected evidence. Stretching the labels to fill the chart's height also wastes space. The large headline-number strip belongs to almost any analytics product; it competes with the actual observational record. At narrow widths the SVG's small lettering becomes unnecessarily difficult to read.

This pass removes the repeated species icons and the masthead badge. A single moth engraving identifies the collection plate, while its three compact, pinned record labels contain only traffic-source information. That preserves the Victorian collecting language without pretending each source is an insect species. The labels now have intrinsic height rather than stretching to match the neighbouring register. Their measurements use the same disciplined numeric typography as the daily observations. The summary is reduced to a restrained ruled ledger so the weather-recorder traces carry the page's visual emphasis. Both ingredients belong to one field-record sheet, with one illustration rather than four competing emblems.

The graph keeps the separate, zero-based visits and signups strips. Its SVG text grows at narrow widths, and line strokes remain legible when the figure scales. The redundant MON—SUN heading annotation is removed because the chart already labels the days. Every supplied daily and source count, weekly total, conversion rate and source share remains visible. No new data, benchmarks, weather readings or historical claims are introduced. The revision is deliberately subtractive: the remaining visual identity comes from the instrument paper, ruled register, engraved specimen and pinned source records.
