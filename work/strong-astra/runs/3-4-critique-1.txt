The existing page is competent but its identity is mostly a costume: beige paper, a gold W, Georgia headings and renamed tables. The W has no meaning. “A week in review” occupies the strongest typographic position without identifying the report. The three KPI cells, full-width tables and repeated legends could belong to almost any dashboard. Calling them a register and ledger does not make bureaucracy shape the composition; adding gold does not make it a luxury hotel.

The revision commits to a single object: a hotel’s weekly administrative folio. A narrow, ruled filing margin carries the period and numbered section titles, while the working column holds the totals and records. The luxury ingredient shapes the proportions, large restrained serif typography, deep green cover band and generous space around tightly set accounts. The bureaucracy ingredient supplies the field labels, persistent alignment, monospaced numeric entries and strong total rules. There is no invented hotel brand or decorative seal.

The vague headline, monogram, repeated legends and duplicated total notes are removed. The page names its actual purpose. Daily and acquisition sections keep every supplied count and existing rounded rate; the visit bars stay inside the Visits column, so no separate legend is necessary. Sunday and Direct receive the same restrained row treatment because they lead their respective tables. Findings move into the filing margin beside the evidence instead of creating a separate banner. Synthetic scope and rounding remain stated once; the lack of a prior period remains explicit. Mobile collapses the filing margin into compact section headers and preserves all four table columns.

This is a change to the information’s architecture and typographic roles, not an accumulation of themed ornaments.
