The existing page uses a familiar dashboard with a government-office costume: a small square logo, numbered badges, a monospace eyebrow, and an end-of-record signoff. None changes how information is organized. The oversized header and separate summary strip push the useful record down. The source mini-bars have no explicit metric and mix percentage widths with a maximum width, so they are more decoration than a dependable comparison. The daily chart has unlabelled gridlines and an arbitrary scale. Its chart and daily register feel like separate widgets rather than two views of the same evidence.

The revision commits to a performance return: a ruled administrative form with a machine-readable numerical register. A serif document title, compact record identifier, numbered section margins, and shared table rules establish the bureaucracy; precise monospace values, a labelled zero-baseline plot, metric controls, and an integrated daily register supply the technology. The daily chart and register are grouped as one section, with traffic sources in a neighboring section. The weak source mini-bars and ornamental status copy are removed. Weekly totals, every supplied daily and source figure, calculated signup rates, and the synthetic-data disclosure remain. Chart switching uses explicit round scales (2,400 visits or 150 signups), with the scale updated alongside the bars. A narrow-screen layout stacks the sections while retaining readable tables and labels. No outside data or historical comparisons are introduced.
