The current design finally has a recognisable visual language, but its information structure is still generic. It splits one seven-day performance into a chart and a separate table, making the reader locate the same day twice. That is especially wasteful when the chart is being presented as a weather observation record. The large lower table also competes with the playbill instead of letting the week read as one composition. Adding more curtains, weather symbols or operatic wording would only disguise that structural weakness.

The revision makes the observation sheet do the work. Visits, signups and conversion now share the same seven day positions: the visit trace sits above two explicitly labelled measurement rows. The daily figures remain exact and visible, while a visually hidden semantic table preserves a straightforward screen-reader equivalent. This is both a weather bulletin's aligned observations and an opera programme's single ordered performance, rather than two themed widgets. The three traffic sources move into one three-column section beneath the weekly performance, becoming comparable at a glance; they stack on narrow screens. The crimson billing, restrained programme typography and blue measurement trace are retained. No new figures, ornaments or metaphors are introduced, and the repeated visible daily table is removed only after its useful information is incorporated into the main reading surface.
