The previous revision does not fully deliver its stated idea. The acquisition section still has the old markup: its heading is hidden, its table bypasses the folio grid, and its closing wrapper has no matching opening element. The most important comparison after the daily figures consequently loses both its label and the document's organizing margin. This is a concrete failure, not a matter of taste.

The header is also too much of a cover page. A forced two-line title and a large dark block make the report feel like hotel marketing before it becomes useful. “Performance register” repeats the title, “Weekly return” repeats the period, and the separate weekly-account heading makes three totals take more space than they warrant. The side rails work only when they consistently carry section identity and an observation.

The revised design keeps one idea: a luxury hotel's administrative folio. It repairs the acquisition section so its title, source finding and exact data use the same ruled layout as the daily register. The totals become a compact opening balance aligned with both tables. The dark masthead is compressed into a letterhead with the week in its filing compartment; the title can wrap naturally. The redundant masthead eyebrow and empty summary section number are removed. Section numbering applies only to the two actual registers.

Luxury now comes from controlled serif proportions, restrained ink, and the measured separation between quiet labels and exact figures. Bureaucracy comes from continuous filing rules, consistent column divisions, monospaced counts and the double rule under totals. Both table sections use fixed matching column proportions, making the shared metric labels easier to scan. Notes remain small and readable, and mobile removes forced heading breaks while keeping every count and rate.

All supplied figures and the existing rounded signup rates remain unchanged. Sunday and Direct remain the highlighted leaders. No new decorative motif or invented data has been added.
