The existing page is a conventional analytics dashboard with a green accent, a novelty name and a receipt pasted underneath. Remove the logo and takeaway panel and almost nothing about its composition suggests either ingredient. The sea influence is particularly shallow: dark teal is a palette, not a visual idea. Rounded KPI cards, a familiar two-column grid and Arial headings could belong to any SaaS product. The receipt has stronger character than the actual information, so the theme is concentrated in an accessory. “This week, served up” consumes the strongest headline without telling the owner anything. The chart also has a technical weakness: its tallest bars can shrink within a flex container once labels and padding are included, compromising its common scale.

The revision commits to an underwater fast-food order board: luminous, condensed menu typography and numbered service-board sections sit above one continuous pale order receipt containing every daily figure. The primary metrics are the board’s large totals; source performance is its comparison menu; the weekly visit chart is a single clearly scaled display. Deep-sea identity comes from the near-black water field, restrained cyan light and an integrated anglerfish/burger silhouette in the masthead, rather than scattered ocean decoration. Fast-food identity shapes the information hierarchy, ruled rows, type and receipt structure. Labels stay literal. All supplied daily and source counts remain visible, with signup rates and the two supported findings retained. Chart columns use one fixed plotting height and exact proportional heights without flex shrink. No invented targets, comparisons or controls are introduced.
