The existing page is a conventional analytics dashboard with a weather icon attached. Its rounded cards, pale gradient wash, generic headline and floating “On the radar” callout could belong to almost any product. “Daily conditions” changes the vocabulary without changing how the information works. Tech is reduced to tiny monospace labels; weather is reduced to a sun and cloud. The repeated Monday–Sunday captions and source footer also spend space restating established context.

Replace that assembly with one visual idea: a digital weather observation station for website activity. A single instrument frame holds the weekly readings, a gridded daily trace and aligned observation table, and a source register. A dark ink masthead, warm chart paper, precise rules and large monospaced readings give the page a technical identity. Weather contributes the station/bulletin composition and observation cadence, rather than invented forecasts or decorative icons. Plain metric labels remain explicit; all seven daily records, all three sources, weekly totals and calculated rates remain available. Source bars share a labeled zero-to-6,000 scale, and the daily plot retains its zero baseline. Short factual observations replace the detached promotional-looking callout. The mobile layout stacks the same instruments and keeps the table readable; the static page requires neither animation nor scripts.
