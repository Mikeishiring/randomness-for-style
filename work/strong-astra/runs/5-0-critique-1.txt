The original is a familiar dashboard wearing an ocean-colored palette. Rounded cards, a glowing logo dot and the invented “Depth / Analytics” brand do almost all the thematic work. Nothing about the layout feels like a deep-sea instrument; the tech ingredient stops at a monospace period label. The daily plot also uses anonymous grid lines, while its violet signup legend conflicts with the cyan signup total and violet rate values. The three metric captions and repeated week label add words without helping interpretation.

Replace the card grid with one vertically organized observation instrument: a shared left index rail, flat measurement bands and a single uninterrupted daily plotting field. The surface becomes darker through the observations, with restrained cyan illumination confined to actual visit measurements. Monospaced numerals, explicit chart scale, aligned readout rows and technical rules supply the technology; the descending composition, dark water tones and luminous bar tips supply the deep sea. This is one visual system rather than additional nautical decoration. White consistently identifies signups and violet identifies rates.

Keep every supplied daily and source figure, all three weekly metrics, the two useful comparisons and the rounding note. Remove redundant metric captions and the unsupported prior-week reminder. Label synthetic data once in the header. Make the chart a semantic table with day headers and directly readable numbers; its bars are supplementary. Retain the source comparison bars, now aligned with visit counts. Both data areas scroll locally on very narrow screens without shrinking their labels. No scripts, external assets, invented observations or interactive controls are needed.
