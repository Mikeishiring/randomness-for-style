The previous design finally had a consistent diner bulletin, but some decisions still served the costume more than the reading. The red header contained a small prose summary beside a large title instead of a crisp high reading. The weather-style profile lacked a visible numeric scale, so its zero-baseline caption asked the reader to trust the picture. The receipt’s full-width bars sat beneath all four columns without identifying which metric their lengths represented. The separate “Direct leads the week” headline repeated the sentence immediately below it.

Refine the existing bulletin rather than add another motif. Replace the header paragraph with a compact weekly-high reading: Sunday, 2,100 visits, 127 signups, and 6.0% signup rate. This gives the weather convention a practical role and the diner menu a clearly priced-special-like hierarchy without relabeling metrics as food. Add a restrained numerical visits scale and two grid guides to the existing graph, preserving its zero baseline and actual daily values. Move source bars into the Visits column of the printed ticket, eliminating decorative full-width strips. Replace the redundant source headline with “314 of 624,” followed by its exact meaning. The result retains every supplied figure, both useful comparisons, and the rate definition. The warm colors, compact sign typography, weekly profile, and receipt remain the sole visual vocabulary; further icons or themed terminology would reduce clarity.
