The previous revision still looks like a modern dashboard with a branded hero pasted on top. Its large burgundy rectangle, left-aligned title and oversized lyre reproduce the familiar product-header pattern. The claim that seven daily columns behave like lyre strings is especially weak: the actual chart draws cell boundaries, not strings. Opera appears in the Roman numerals, but the composition does not yet resemble a coherent programme. The repeated “A weekly performance” line adds no information. On phones, a fixed 530-pixel chart makes the owner scroll to compare the week, despite there being only seven observations.

This pass replaces the hero treatment with a single continuous printed-programme composition. Orpheus becomes a centered, widely spaced monumental title between fine double rules; the small lyre acts as its publisher-like emblem rather than a competing illustration. The masthead and numerical register now share the same paper, typography and rule system. The redundant subtitle is removed. This is a deliberate narrowing of the visual idea, not a new layer of theatrical decoration.

The chart's seven vertical centerlines now genuinely form strings behind the seven visit bars. Its double-rule baseline belongs to the programme's typographic system. Bars remain straightforward, zero-based and consistently coloured; all exact daily counts remain visible. A compact mobile grid replaces the unnecessary fixed-width chart, with scrolling retained only below 300 pixels. Totals, source detail, findings, rate definition, one-decimal rounding and synthetic scope remain intact. The source table is deliberately kept legible rather than embellished into a metaphor that would make comparison harder.
