The previous revision overstates its own commitment. The supposed timing board is still a conventional vertical bar chart, and the source receipt is still three dashboard progress bars. A dashed separator cannot carry an entire fast-food identity. The oval logo belongs to a broad retro-diner vocabulary, while the racing vocabulary has nearly disappeared. “This week’s numbers” consumes a heading row without adding information already supplied by the period and metrics. Most damagingly, the owner must switch the chart and open a disclosure to compare visits, signups and conversion on the same day. The design is simple to look at but unnecessarily fragmented to use.

Replace that arrangement with one pit-stop counter board: a compact masthead carrying the actual week, a red menu-price strip for the three totals, a dark daily timing ledger, and a cream till receipt for traffic sources. Use the timing ledger’s row alignment, large tabular figures and yellow day markers to do the motorsport work, and the total strip, ruled receipt and condensed counter-sign lettering to do the fast-food work. Remove the oval badge, generic headline, metric switch and redundant collapsed daily table. All seven days now expose all three measures at once, with inline visits bars on a common zero-to-2,400 scale. The table is the visualization rather than a second hidden copy of it.

Keep every original daily/source figure and rounded conversion rate. Keep the total labels literal. Separate the data-heavy dark panel from the light source receipt through structure and contrast, not extra ornaments. Retain source bars and their stated scale, but let exact counts and aligned receipt rows lead. Short adjacent findings identify the Sunday peak and Direct/Social conversion difference. Stack the two surfaces on narrow screens and scale the timing typography so all four columns remain visible. No decorative controls or motion are necessary. This pass replaces the weak central presentation instead of layering another theme onto it.
