The first version is usable, but the identity is thinner than it looks. A checkerboard, burgundy panel and serif headline are familiar shorthand. Motorsport gets a literal symbol; opera gets little beyond the typeface and the phrase “final act.” The result could easily be a luxury dashboard with racing decoration attached. The repeated section numbers and “full running order” title add more theme than meaning.

The strongest decisions deserve preservation: three clear weekly totals, a single switchable daily chart, all seven daily rows, source counts alongside conversion, restrained color and a clear synthetic-data disclosure. The Sunday and Direct findings are supported and concise. There is no reason to introduce more metrics or more explanatory copy.

Clarity has two concrete weaknesses. The chart's evenly spaced horizontal rules have no scale labels, suggesting precision they do not supply. Its bar heights also share a flex container with labels and gaps: at narrow heights, the largest bar can shrink, compromising proportions. The themed daily-table title slows recognition.

Refinement: turn the summary panel into a restrained theatrical proscenium, retaining one slanted racing stripe at its edge. Give “final act” a deliberate italic treatment rather than relying on generic serif styling. Remove redundant checkered decoration from the winning bar; red already identifies Sunday. Remove unlabeled chart gridlines, preserve the zero baseline and exact values, and explicitly prevent bar shrinkage while reserving space for labels. Rename the daily table “Daily detail.” Retain every supplied figure and existing working control. These are targeted changes to the same composition, not a replacement concept.
