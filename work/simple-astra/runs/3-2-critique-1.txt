Originality: The red-and-cream stripe, mustard badge and generic “Department of digital service” are familiar costume pieces. They suggest a diner and an office separately, but do not establish a convincing shared visual system. The rotated week badge is decorative rather than useful. “Daily service” also borrows atmosphere at the expense of a precise section label.

Clarity: The weekly totals, shared-zero visit bars, complete source table and restrained palette are strong. Keep them. The daily signup figures and percentages are detached from their labels; the reader must read a sentence below the chart and map its slash notation back to two rows. The small source bars have no visible scale or metric label and add ambiguity to an otherwise clear table. Sunday’s callout repeats three already visible values when its useful contribution is identifying that all three peaked together. The large title and repeated administrative phrasing use space without helping the owner assess performance.

Refinement: Develop the shared ingredient through a restrained service-counter return form: a flat numbered week ticket, ruled metric cells, a perforated daily section boundary and compact monospaced form labels. Keep the existing red, mustard and paper palette and the visit chart. Use explicit row headings for daily signups and rates, with a horizontally scrollable minimum width on narrow screens so all seven days remain legible. Remove the ambiguous source microbars. Use plain section names and shorter findings while retaining every supplied figure, calculated rate, provenance and rounding note. Keep the three summary metrics and full source table. No new claims, controls or data are introduced.
