Originality: The numbered ticket, perforation, warm paper and ruled cells now form a coherent service-counter document. It still relies on familiar red-and-mustard shorthand, but adding food icons, stamps or further invented office language would make this small report more theatrical rather than more distinctive. Preserve the existing system. The weakest identity choice is now the headline: “Weekly service return” sounds like a form the owner has to submit and relegates the actual purpose to small supporting text.

Clarity: Explicit row labels and semantic tables are a substantial improvement. The remaining visit legend repeats the row heading and consumes space. On mobile, horizontal scrolling can move the daily metric names out of sight, defeating the improvement. The source table reserves 42 percent of its width for three short names, unnecessarily squeezing its numeric columns. These are concrete defects worth fixing; another wholesale restyling is not.

Refinement: Make “Website performance” the sole headline while retaining the numbered week ticket and form styling. Remove the redundant visit legend. Keep daily metric headings visible while scrolling and make the column boundary apparent. Allocate source columns according to their contents, with a horizontally scrollable, keyboard-focusable fallback for very narrow screens; use source names as semantic row headings. Preserve all counts, rates, finding sentences, chart proportions and provenance. No additional decorative elements or claims are needed.
