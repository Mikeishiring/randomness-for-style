The first design is readable, but its originality is thin. A navy dashboard, mint gradients and a glowing dot could represent almost any technology product; “Depth” supplies more ocean identity than the actual composition. Adding fish, bubbles or more glow would only make that weakness louder. The better direction is restrained depth contours and precise instrument-like rules, confined to the framing.

The information design has stronger foundations: correct totals, directly labelled daily values, honest zero-baseline bars, a compact source table, and useful comparisons. Preserve all of those. The most obvious clarity fault is color: signups are mint in the headline but violet in the chart, while signup rate also uses violet. The chart legend also looks like two plotted series even though signups appear only as counts. Source bars lack an explicit metric, and “RATE” is unnecessarily vague. Unlabelled chart grid lines imply a scale without providing one.

This refinement gives visits a consistent mint and signups a consistent violet, keeps rates neutral, explicitly distinguishes visit bars from signup counts, removes the unlabelled grid, and names the source rate. It removes the source mini-bars because their already labelled visit totals provide the same comparison without an ambiguous second encoding. A quiet contour field and fine technical framing develop the same deep-sea / tech identity without covering the data. Redundant total-card copy and an unsupported-comparison disclaimer are removed; the rate formula, synthetic-data disclosure, rounding rule, all supplied values and both substantive interpretations remain. No interaction or invented metric is added.
