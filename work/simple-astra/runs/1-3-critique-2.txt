The revised design is clearer, but it still hides two avoidable compromises. First, the traffic-source bars are attached to source names even though their lengths encode visits. The reader has to infer that relationship across the table. Second, the mobile fix made chart values and explanatory text as small as nine pixels. Fitting all the data is not the same as making it readable.

Originality is now concentrated in a credible small device: Orpheus’s lyre sits inside an opera proscenium, while the double-rule masthead and typography suggest a printed programme. The rest is deliberately conventional analytics. That is an appropriate boundary for this brief. Adding masks, curtains across the chart, mythological labels or more theatrical wording would turn identity into interference. Preserve the emblem, programme framing, burgundy palette and straightforward headings.

Refinement: move each proportional traffic-source bar directly beneath its visit count, making the encoding self-evident while retaining all values and comparisons. Use actual row headers for source names. Separate Week 01 and Synthetic data in the existing masthead so the period can be read immediately. Raise small-screen visit labels, metric labels and the rate definition to eleven pixels; reclaim space through slightly narrower page margins and chart gaps. Keep the exact daily and source data, totals, rates, observations, accessible chart description and rate definition. No additional metric, external source or decorative section is introduced.
