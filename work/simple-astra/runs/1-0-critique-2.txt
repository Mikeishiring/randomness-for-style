The previous revision has a coherent identity now, but it is still restrained rather than radically original: classical serif numerals and a temple are familiar shorthand, and the circuit-column mark carries most of the combination. That is an acceptable limit for this report. Expanding the mythology into section names, ornamental charts, or narrative copy would make the owner work harder to interpret ordinary website data. The Oracle name and integrated mark are the strongest identity decisions and should stay.

The remaining clarity defects are practical. On small screens, chart gaps shrink to 7px while weekday gaps remain 12px, shifting the labels relative to their bars. Metric buttons are small for touch. The source heading and its denominator compete for width at intermediate viewport sizes. The four-column daily table has no explicit narrow-screen overflow handling. The leftover meander styling is unused, and the height transition does not animate the replaced bars: each switch rebuilds the elements.

This pass fixes those defects without inventing a new visual concept. Match weekday and bar gaps, give metric controls 44px touch targets, allow the source heading to wrap as a group while keeping its denominator intact, and contain the full daily table in a named, keyboard-focusable horizontal scrolling region only when needed. Remove the unused ornament and ineffective transition rules. Preserve the existing identity, all supplied data, computed shares, conversions, findings, metric controls, and daily detail. No further decorative development is justified: it would compete with information rather than improve the combination.

This is one refinement pass based on the saved HTML. No external sources or browser inspection were used.
