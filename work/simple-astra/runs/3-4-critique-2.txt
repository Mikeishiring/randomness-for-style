The second result is coherent, but it still spends too much of its opening on administrative theater. “Website / Performance Register,” “Weekly Return,” and “Website performance” repeat essentially the same information. The boxed monogram, gold rule, double rules, and period field already establish a formal hotel folio. More institutional wording does not make that combination more original.

The useful identity now lives in the document itself: the stationery treatment, restrained brass accents, serif display type, and ruled ledger. Keep that. Inventing a hotel name, stamping approvals, or replacing analytics terminology with hospitality metaphors would make the report less honest and less legible. Originality has reached the point where additional decoration would be a regression.

The more concrete weakness is small-screen typography. The previous refinement shrinks some labels to eight or nine pixels to preserve the desktop composition. That sacrifices the report to its stationery. A short stacked heading is preferable to unreadable administrative labels. Also, the stylesheet has accumulated overlapping rules and an early print rule that later rules partially override; the resulting behavior is needlessly fragile.

Refine only these weaknesses: simplify the masthead to “Weekly register” and one synthetic-data disclosure, retain the clear subject title and period, make mobile labels readable, and let the period occupy a compact horizontal row below the title on narrow screens. Consolidate the stylesheet and place print styles last. Retain every count, rate, section, bar scale, highlighted leader, finding, rate definition, and comparison limitation. Keep the same desktop visual hierarchy and independent table scales. Give each table an accessible name. No further aesthetic change is justified by the brief.
