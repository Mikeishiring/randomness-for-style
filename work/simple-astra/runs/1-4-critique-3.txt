The design is restrained rather than spectacular. Most of its structure is still a familiar performance dashboard: three totals, a daily chart and a source table. The Greek-myth identity is concentrated in the Ariadne name and thread-and-labyrinth mark; someone unfamiliar with the myth will see an elegant geometric emblem rather than recognize the reference. The luxury-hotel identity relies on register typography, quiet spacing, ivory, green and gold rather than literal hotel imagery. Those are real limits to its originality.

They are not sufficient reasons to add more decoration. The paired ingredients now have one coherent expression: Ariadne's thread guides a quiet weekly register. More literal mythology would require explanation or distract from the figures. Hotel terminology has already been removed from analytic labels where it could confuse website visits with physical guests. Reintroducing it would be a regression.

The information hierarchy is now direct: Week 01, weekly totals, daily distribution, traffic sources and the weakest source's signup rate. The daily bars expose exact values, and source bars sit beneath their actual visits counts. The supplied daily signup detail remains available through a clearly labeled toggle. Percentages are defined and rounded consistently, and synthetic status is disclosed once. The brief findings interpret different useful comparisons without inventing causes or a prior period.

I find no further justified design change under the instruction to change only what improves the result. Additional ornament would compete with the information; additional prose would explain an identity that should remain quiet; removing more detail would reduce usefulness. The saved HTML is an identical copy of the previous result. This is an intentional stopping point, not a claim that the design is maximally original.
