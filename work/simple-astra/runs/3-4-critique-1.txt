The first result is clear and numerically sound, but its identity is mostly a palette and a serif font. A boxed W is an arbitrary monogram; it does little to connect a luxury hotel to an administrative register. “A week in review” is generic editorial copy. The bureaucracy is expressed through repeated labels rather than through the structure of the document. The hotel ingredient needs restraint and considered proportions, not more ornament.

The strongest decisions are the warm paper, dark green ink, brass accent, double rules, three unambiguous totals, and tables that retain every supplied count. Keep those. The Sunday highlight usefully locates the leading day. All totals and rounded rates are correct.

Clarity has several avoidable costs. The two tables assign different widths and different colors to the same measure, visits. Repeated bar legends explain something the column heading can express; meanwhile, the independent bar scales are unstated. The metric notes repeat the scope already established by the weekly document and its source table. The masthead repeats the week, the heading postpones the actual subject, and the footer's “END OF RETURN” adds ceremony without information. On a narrow screen the decorative bars compete with the exact numbers.

Refinement: retain the register but treat it as a carefully typeset hotel folio. Give the document a direct title and a ruled period field; retain the small monogram as a quiet stationery detail. Use a restrained brass top rule, consistent tabular numerals, stronger table structure, and matching column widths. Keep visits green in both tables and state each bar scale beside its section. Remove redundant metric notes and ceremonial footer text, retaining the rate definition, synthetic provenance, rounding, and lack of prior-period comparison. Give Direct the same restrained row emphasis as Sunday, linking both parts of the existing finding to their evidence. At small widths remove only the redundant bars, preserving all numbers and rates. No figures, purpose, or useful detail change.
