The first result is a competent ledger with a costume attached. Forest green, cream, gold rules and Georgia convey a conventional luxury hotel; the tiny squid crest does almost all the Cthulhu work. Its cross-like ornament reads more ecclesiastical than cosmic, while “A residence in the deep” explains an identity the drawing should establish. More tentacles across the report would make the weakness louder, not fix it.

Clarity is mostly strong: totals are correct, the daily table preserves every supplied observation, conversion is calculated consistently, and the responsive two-column layout is appropriate. Those decisions should stay. The source mini-bars are placed beneath names without identifying what they measure. Sunday alone changes the visits color without a legend. Chart grid lines have no labeled scale, while “zero baseline” is unnecessary interface commentary. The two findings repeat data already nearby, and the footer repeats the brand.

This refinement develops one concentrated identity: an arched hotel seal housing an unmistakably tentacled, winged sea god, framed by restrained brass lines. “Hotel & abyss” names the two ingredients briefly. The reporting area stays plain. Headings become Daily performance and Traffic sources; all daily visits use one color; unlabeled grid lines disappear; bars explicitly resist flex shrinking so heights retain their proportional scale. Traffic-source bars move immediately beneath their visit values and share a right-aligned baseline. Conversion is spelled out in both tables. Duplicate explanatory copy and the repeated footer brand are removed, while all daily and source figures, conversion values, findings, period and synthetic-data attribution remain. No external figures or comparisons were introduced.
