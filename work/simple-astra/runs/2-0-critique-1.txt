Originality: The first result is competent but generic. Pale blue cards, a sun-and-cloud icon, and monospace labels barely establish Weather × Tech. Remove the logo and it could be almost any analytics dashboard. “Daily conditions” is a decorative metaphor that actually weakens the report: the reader has to translate it back into visits. The invented status-like dot next to “On the radar” also adds instrument styling without conveying a real state.

Clarity: The numerical hierarchy and full daily table are strong. The totals reconcile, the chart begins at zero, and the source comparisons use consistent scales. Those decisions should stay. “Your week in view” wastes the most prominent line on a generic promise. The chart needs an immediately readable metric label, especially when separated from its heading. The pale line chart also lacks visual priority relative to the three equally weighted summary cards. The Sunday row is bold without enough spatial distinction. The no-comparison limitation is useful but buried in a tiny footer.

One refinement: Replace the generic headline with the supported within-week finding. Give the existing daily chart the appearance of a weather-station readout using a restrained dark atmospheric field, a fine observation grid, high-contrast plotted readings, and a plain Visits label. Keep the zero baseline, every plotted value, and all underlying data. Replace the ambiguous section heading with “Visits by day.” Give Sunday a subtle row background, replace the status-like dot with a simple instrument rule, and place the lack of prior-week data beside the observations. Consolidate the rate calculation and rounding note in the footer. Preserve the light table and source panel so the weather-station identity remains concentrated in the primary chart rather than spreading decoration across every component.
