The design is not radically original. Its body remains a conventional analytics report, and burgundy, cream and serif type alone would be a generic cultural-institution aesthetic. The distinct work happens in the masthead: the lyre inside a proscenium connects the Orpheus identity to opera in one restrained emblem. The programme frame and Roman section numbers support that connection. This is enough identity for the task; demanding a more conspicuous theme would risk making ordinary website measures feel like a puzzle.

Clarity is now materially stronger than novelty. The title says what the report is, the week and synthetic status are visible together, the three totals lead, and the daily chart preserves exact visits and signup counts. Source bars sit directly under the counts they encode. Source names are semantic row headers. The two observations identify actual leaders without pretending there is a previous-period comparison or a causal explanation. The rate definition is consolidated once.

There are remaining aesthetic compromises: the branding occupies more vertical space than a utilitarian dashboard needs, source bars are small, and eleven-pixel secondary text is still modest on a narrow screen. None justifies a further unverified change in this pass. Removing the programme masthead would weaken the requested identity; enlarging the microbars would consume table space without adding information; expanding every small label would pressure the seven-day layout. The repeated CSS overrides are untidy implementation, but rewriting them would not by itself improve the rendered design and could introduce regressions.

No design change is justified by this critique. Preserve the existing balance instead of inventing another decorative iteration. The complete HTML is saved byte-for-byte unchanged, retaining the purpose, all supplied data, calculated rates and useful detail.
