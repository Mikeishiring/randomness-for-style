Originality: the design is not radically original. Its basic structure remains the familiar totals–chart–table report, and dark teal plus serif typography alone would be a weak answer to the brief. The custom proscenium/jellyfish mark is the decisive distinction: it combines the two ingredients in one drawn object. The arched brass hairline and luminous bar endpoints extend that distinction without turning metrics into scenery. Demanding a more theatrical layout now would reward novelty at the expense of the owner's task.

Clarity: the title identifies the report; Week 01 and synthetic status are visible; the totals are correct; daily values have direct labels and an explicit baseline; the switch names its metric; source counts and conversion rates sit together. The daily table preserves every supplied count and adds transparent derived rates. The Sunday finding gives one useful interpretation rather than narrating every row. The collapsed table is a deliberate tradeoff: simultaneous daily comparison takes one extra action, but exposing it by default would duplicate the chart and lengthen the overview.

The remaining aesthetic limitations are acceptable. The gold and green relationship is conventional, the chart is conventional, and the opera reference is restrained. None constitutes a clarity defect. Extra imagery, additional accents, motion, a more eccentric type treatment or expanded commentary would compete with this small dataset. Previous passes already addressed the justified layout and accessibility issues. No further change has a demonstrated benefit under “Change only what improves it.” The complete HTML is therefore saved unchanged, byte for byte, as 5-3-4.html. This is a source-level critique, not a claim of browser-rendered verification.
