The design is not radically original. Cream paper, serif typography, ruled charts, and specimen labels are familiar archival conventions. The pinned source records are an allusion to Victorian collecting rather than a literal insect collection, and the weather identity rests mostly on the instrument dial and graph-paper trace. Those limitations are real. They do not justify turning website analytics into a fictional natural-history exhibition.

The two ingredients already share a coherent observational language: an engraved insect inside a dial, a meteorograph-like weekly trace, and source records presented as specimen labels. Adding insect silhouettes to individual traffic sources would imply an arbitrary classification. Adding clouds or storms to days would imply weather or performance judgments absent from the supplied data. Replacing direct metric names with period terminology would make the report harder to understand. None is an improvement.

Clarity is now the stronger part of the page. Weekly totals lead; the Sunday finding names its measures; the daily trace retains a zero baseline and exact values; the table preserves all daily signups and conversion rates; the three sources use a shared share-of-visits scale with exact counts and conversion. Synthetic status, the conversion denominator, rounding, and the absent comparison period remain explicit. The previous pass addressed narrow-screen typography, early column stacking, and loss of the insect seal on phones.

No additional change is justified in this pass. Further ornament would compete with information, while further cutting would remove useful detail or the remaining identity. The revised HTML is therefore an identical copy of the preceding result. This is a deliberate stop under “Change only what improves it,” not a claim that the design is unprecedented.
