The second version has finally earned its name: the mark combines a proscenium with a jellyfish, while the brass hairline, serif headings and luminous green plotting establish the two ingredients without theatrical labels obscuring the metrics. Adding curtains, sea creatures or more stage terminology would make a website report worse. Keep that restraint.

The remaining weakness is presentation consistency. The signup-rate definition is an inline appendage to a large numeral; at intermediate widths it can wrap unpredictably. That gives one metric an accidental layout while the others remain orderly. Small-screen section controls and headings can also consume more width than they have. The daily table repairs data availability, but the visual chart still announces unlabeled numbers before their days to assistive technology. With scripting disabled, it leaves an empty chart and controls that cannot work.

Refine those specific issues, not the entire identity. Give every summary value a stable line and place the short rate definition beneath its number. Allow section headers to wrap and keep figures proportionate on narrow screens. Make every plotted bar a labeled accessible image with its day, metric and value; announce the selected chart metric. Hide the unused visual chart and switch without JavaScript, leaving the complete daily table available. Retain the custom mark, arch, brass detailing, ocean palette, direct labels and all supplied data. No additional decorative device is justified.
